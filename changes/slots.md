A worker can run two training jobs at once (`--slots N`), and `worker calibrate` finds out whether a machine should: it trains a fresh brain with 128 notes of memory in the tree world (`configs/nursery-tree-home.toml`, the kind of run our lines are) alone for a couple of minutes, then two at once, and picks two slots when the pair trains at least 1.2 times as fast and fits in under 60% of the RAM (80% of the GPU's memory), else one. Brains with memory spend most of their time in updates, so they leave room for a second job where a plain brain in a tiny world doesn't; `--world`, `--train` and `--memory` measure something else. `worker run` uses the saved answer unless `--slots` says otherwise and logs which it used. A score job still takes the whole machine: it starts only on an empty one, and nothing else is claimed while it runs. Each run's `job.json` records how many jobs ran beside it (`beside`, worker version 12). `terrarium.train` takes `--root` for where the run folder goes.
