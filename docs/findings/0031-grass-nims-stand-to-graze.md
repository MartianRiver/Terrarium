# 0031 The grass nims keep to one patch to graze once hunger fades when full and grass feeds only a near-stopped nim: 80–90% of their meadow time within about a body width of one spot on both lines of brains, the pond brain's line too; whether a turn cost helps is lost in seed luck, and measured by speed none seemed to stop

date: 2026-11-10 · status: confirmed · seeds: 9,500,000 × 64 (probe used for reading each rung); 9,710,000 × 64, also used to read the later rungs and controls; confirmed on 9,730,000 × 64, never used for choosing or reading; training seeds 934 to 953 · brains: g1b-fresh-s1 (start, 0029), g1d-fresh-s1, g1e-fresh-h1-s1, g1f-fresh-s1, g1g-fresh-s1, g1h-turn-fresh-s1 and -s2, g1i-fresh-s1 to -s4, g1j-more-fresh-s1 and -s2, g1k-more-fresh-s1, g1k-025-fresh-s1 and -s2, g1l-ent-fresh-s1, g1k-more-fresh-l2 and g1k-025-fresh-l2 (from 1j-s2); controls g1hc-ctl-fresh-s1 and -s2 (1g + 6M, turning free) and g1ic-ctl-fresh-s1 (1h + 6M at still 1); the pond brain's line g1i-sc2-s1 (6M each)

**Question.** 0029 left the grass nims running the meadows at full speed though grazing paid more the slower they went. 0030 found the pond nims stop once the reward stops paying for turnover and hunger is felt all the time. Do the grass nims stand to graze on the same reward, and what else does it take?

**World and brains.** 0029's grazing world: one nim, 90 s games, two meadows of 1.2 m radius near home, grass the only food, `[grass] still` 4 (a bite at speed v is worth `clip(1 − v/4)` of a stopped one). The fresh brain's line, each rung one change from the one before, 6M from its brain:
- 1d, `needs_lost` 1 → 6.
- 1e, `[reward] hunger` 1 (a cost of (1 − energy)² a second, nothing when full).
- 1f, `[grass] still` 4 → 2; 1g, still 2 → 1.
- 1h, `[energy] turn` 0 → 0.02 (a full turn costs what full thrust does).
- 1i, still 1 → 0.5.
- 1j, nothing: 6M more of 1i in its world.
- From 1j, on the same seed: 1k-more (nothing, 6M more), 1k-025 (still 0.5 → 0.25) and 1l (`[ppo] entropy` 0 → −0.01, a pull on the action spread).

1h, 1i, 1j and 1k-025 were trained again on a second seed (1h and 1i from the same parent; 1j-s2 from 1i-s2; 1k-025-s2 from 1j).

Controls, each the rung before with 6M more and nothing changed, on the checked rung's own training seed: 1hc (1g + 6M with turning free, against 1h-s1 and 1h-s2 on their seeds) and 1ic (1h-s1 + 6M at still 1, against 1i-s1). And the pond brain's line (0028's sc2, which with the turn cost still turned at the clip on every choice) took rung 1i the same way (g1i-sc2).

**Method.** Two frozen probes over 64 sampled games (as in training), last checkpoints. `grass_probe_v2.py`: speed in a meadow, grazing. `anchor_probe.py`: a moment is anchored when, for the next 3 s, the nim stays within 0.25 m of where it stood, inside a meadow; overlapping moments join into stretches, and each stretch's extent is the diagonal of the box around everywhere it went (chained windows would let a crawl under 0.08 m/s count while it travels, so the extent is what says whether a stretch stays on one patch). The nim is 0.9 m across and a meadow 2.4 m. Both blocks are fresh to this measure; the confirm block was never used for choosing. The first measure of stillness we used, under 0.1 m/s for 2 s, found no stop at all in any rung: the late nims shuffle in place, so their speed flicks above 0.1 every second or two while they stay on the patch.

**What it rules out.** That the grass nims can't keep still enough to graze, or that grass can't ask for it: from 1i on every line spends from a third to four fifths of its meadow time anchored (38 to 83% over four seeds, stretches of 6 to 8 s typically), and after 6M more 80 to 94% (stretches of 7 to 14 s typically, up to the whole game), each stretch within about a body width of one spot (extent median 0.4 to 0.6 m, p90 under 1.2 m). They shuffle on a patch rather than stand dead still: stretches inside half a metre are only 11 to 26% of meadow time. That the reward fix is enough on its own here, as it nearly was at the pond: needs_lost 6 and hunger leave them racing (1d) and then circling at full turn in loops about half a metre across, fed and full (1e), because grazing is capped by room in the belly, so a full nim pays nothing for moving, and turning was free. That tighter grass alone stands them: still 2 and 1 slow them into slow loops and then pirouettes on the spot, turning at the clip on 64 to 72% of choices (1f, 1g). What kept them on a patch: grass that feeds only a near-stopped nim (1i, still 0.5) beat its control on every seed (38 to 83% anchored over four seeds, against 17% for 1h trained 6M more at still 1), and more training settled it (1j, about 90% on one line; the other a rung later: 1j-s2 fell from 38 to 25%, then 6M more again took it to 80%, and grass that feeds only under 0.25 m/s to 89%). Whether the turn cost helped, it can't say: from 1g, 6M more with the cost stood 4.8 and 56.6% on two seeds, and 6M more with turning free 66.8 and 4.4% on the same two seeds, so about one run in two finds standing either way and the pirouettes of 1g are a stage that more training can end without a price on turning. On the settled lines, a pull on the spread (1l) adds a few points at most, and grass that feeds only under 0.25 m/s adds a few on the first line and ten on the second (80 → 89%). That only the fresh brain's line gets there: the pond brain's line, which kept turning at the clip on every choice with the turn cost, stands 80% of its meadow time at rung 1i.

It also rules out the speed bar as a measure of standing still for these nims: under 0.1 m/s for 2 s found none in any rung, including those that keep to one patch 90% of the time.

**Limits.** Two lines of brains: the fresh brain's and the pond brain's (one seed). The colony brain's line collapsed at rung 1f (still 2): all its nims starve, and its steering spread is 0.03 against the fresh line's 0.35, so it can't try going slow; that is a training limit (the spread has no floor): reset, it stands at rung 1f (0032). Seeds part by 50 points or more at 1h and its control, so single-seed comparisons on this ladder mean little. The 0.25 m and 3 s of the anchored measure were chosen once, before reading, and not varied; grazing isn't part of it, so standing on a cell already eaten bare counts (played by its mean actions instead of sampled, 1i spends a third of its time on bare cells and grazes a third less, so steering by the mean is not better grazing). Stretches are counted per nim and games differ, so the counts are clustered by game. The second seeds of 1h and 1i differ a lot (4.8 against 56.6%, 68.1 against 37.6%), and the two lines part after 1i: 6M more took the first from 68 to 89% and the second from 38 to 25%. The second line got there with one more 6M chunk (80%) or with still 0.25 (89%), so standing holds on both lines, but when it arrives is loose. Two lines, one world, and the anchored measure is new; each rung also added 6M of training, and the controls (1hc, 1ic) test only two of the changes, so for the others these are what this ladder did, not proof each change was needed. One nim, energy only, 90 s games, drain 0.01 a second: a game needs about two cells of grass, so they can stand on one spot till it's bare and stay full, which makes standing cheap; a faster drain (1m) asks whether they move on to fresh grass. The hunger term, the turn cost and the starvation fix are on the grass-needs branch, not on next.
