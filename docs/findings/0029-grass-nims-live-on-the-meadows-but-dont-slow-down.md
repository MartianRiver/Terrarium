# 0029 The grass nims learn to live on the meadows, but at full speed: making grazing pay more the slower they go didn't slow them down, nor did a pull on the thrust

date: 2026-11-09 · status: hint · seeds: 9,500,000 × 64 (probe, sampled play, one block for every brain); training seeds 931 (1a), 932 (1b), 933 (1c) · brains: g1a-c29-s1, g1a-sc2-s1, g1a-fresh-s1 (6M each, still 0); g1b-c29-s1, g1b-sc2-s1, g1b-fresh-s1 (6M each from its 1a brain, still 4); g1c-fresh-b01-s1, g1c-sc2-b01-s1, g1c-fresh-b1-s1 (6M each from its 1b brain, still 4, thrust bound 0.01 / 0.01 / 0.1); parents c29-from18-s1 (a colony brain) and sc2-e12-s2 (the pond brain of 0028)

**Question.** 0028 couldn't teach the pond nims to stop, and found nothing in their world asks for it except a pond crossed in about 2 s. So give them a world where the food itself takes slowing down: grass, the only food, that a nim eats where it stands. Can they learn to live on grass at all (rung 1a, grazing at any speed), does a colony brain, which already rests at home, start better than a fresh one, and once grazing pays more the slower they go (rung 1b) do they slow down on the meadows?

**World and brains.** The grass nursery: one nim in an 8 m arena, 90 s games, two meadows of radius 1.2 m, 2.5 m from the nest, grass the only food, no water, nights, hatching or tree. A cell holds 1 cover, a nim takes 0.25 of it a second for 0.4 energy a unit (so a full cell feeds it 0.1 energy a second for 4 s) and it grows back over 50 s. Staying alive costs 0.01 energy a second and full thrust 0.02. `[grass] still` is the speed where a bite stops yielding anything: 0 for 1a (a nim grazes at any speed, so being in a meadow feeds it), 4 for 1b (a bite at 2 m/s, top speed, is worth half, a stop is worth all). That is the world's only change from 1a to 1b, with 6M samples each, each brain from its own 1a brain; the training seed moved from 931 to 932, and the fresh brain's learning rate from 3e-4 in 1a to the fine-tune rate 5e-5 in 1b, so for fresh 1b is two changes. 1c keeps 1b's world and recipe and adds one thing: `[ppo] bound`, a training-loss pull bringing the thrust mean back inside the clip (0028's sc7). Three starts for 1a: c29-from18-s1 (the colony brain that already rests, 0028), sc2-e12-s2 (the pond brain from 0028's ladder) and a fresh brain (the default learning rate 3e-4; the other two 5e-5). The reward is W7c's (`needs` 6, death −1, no effort cost). Grazing pays at once, inside the ~20 s horizon.

**Method.** A grass probe (`grass_probe.py`, 64 games from seed 9,500,000, sampled play as in training) of each brain's last checkpoint: seconds alive of 90, how many of 64 died, the share of time on grass, bites a living minute, speed on grass (10th to 90th percentile), stops of 2 s or more on grass, and what the grazing would have kept at each `still` (the share of what it ate that it would keep). 1a brains are probed in the 1a world and the 1a parents also in the still-4 world beside the 1b brains. Zero-shot rows are the untrained starting brains in the 1a world.

**Result.** Seconds alive of 90, deaths, time on grass, bites a minute, speed on grass, and what grazing would keep at still 4 / still 1:

The would-keep column is left out (–) for the still-4 rows: there the probe applied the speed discount on top of grazing the world had already discounted, so those numbers were wrong. In every trained row a stop of 2 s on grass happens 0 times a living minute (random play stops 0.27 times a minute). In 1a all three learn to live on the meadows in 6M, and the colony brain is no better a parent than a fresh one: both alive 90.0 of 90 s with no deaths, 28% of the time on grass and 1.70 against 1.69 bites a minute. The pond brain sc2, which was starved and slow zero-shot (55.5 s, 17% on grass), gets to 86.4 s and 33% on grass and keeps its slowness in part (its 10th percentile speed is 0.76 m/s after 1a, 0.73 in the still-4 world). In 1b, which makes slowing down pay, none of them slows down: speed on grass is 1.894 to 1.896 m/s for c29 and 1.894 to 1.895 for sc2, a flat top speed on grass, and 1.90 to 1.99 for fresh; sc2 re-pinned from a 10th percentile of 0.73 to 1.894, and what grazing would keep at still 1 is about 1%, 0.6% and 0. They live longer in the still-4 world mainly by spending more of their time on grass (33 / 39 / 52% → 50 / 52 / 57%, bites a minute 1.05 / 1.36 / 1.59 → 1.58 / 1.67 / 1.75; c29's deaths fell from 53 to 15, sc2's from 13 to 2).

In 1c the pull brings the thrust mean down (its training loss falls from 2.98 to 0.74 at 0.01 and from 2.95 to 0.13 at 0.1) but never inside the clip: 96 to 100% of choices stay past it all run, and speed on grass moves only at fresh-b1's 10th percentile (1.90 → 1.59 m/s).

**What it rules out.** That the grass world is a hard one: all three starts live on it by 6M, and nothing asks for stopping at still 0 (none stops; c29 and fresh are pinned at the clip, sc2 isn't). That starting from the colony brain helps: it matches a fresh brain on every measure, though at zero-shot it was the better starter (76.3 s against 55.5 for sc2 and 72.5 for random play). That a price on speed in the food itself reaches a pinned thrust: at still 4 a pinned nim still gets half the grazing at full speed, so every draw it makes pays the same, and the gain from 1b went to finding the grass faster and staying on it, not to stopping (fresh already lived 90 of 90 s before it, so for it only the time on grass moved). The likely reading, from 1b's flat speeds and not measured on the thrust itself, is 0028's sc1 again on grass: a price can't teach an action the policy gradient can't see move.

**Limits.** Every rung used needs 6 against needs_lost 1 with no effort cost, and a review after these runs found that this pays for turnover: burning energy and grazing it back nets about 5× what it cost, so the reward itself may favour speed; rung 1d (needs_lost 6) tests that. The world also had a starvation bug: energy was clipped at 0 before grazing was added, so a nim slowly starving on a bare cell could live on regrowth, and survival here may read high. One seed a run, three runs a rung, one probe block of 64 games, hints. The seed blocks differ a lot at this size: a separate 32-game block (seed 1,000,000) read c29 zero-shot at 59 s where the probe block gave 76.3, so compare only on the probe's. Each 1b brain has 12M samples in all (6M in 1a, 6M in 1b). A nim that grazes at full speed still lives here, so this world doesn't punish speed hard; a still of 1 would. Still 1 (grazing slower than a walk) wasn't tried: it is the world where being pinned costs most, and 1b's still 4 was chosen so the old way kept partly paying (0028's sc4 lesson). Not measured here: the grazing nims' thrust mean and spread directly (the speeds are the evidence they are pinned), and whether any 1b brain's spread would sample a stop.

**Rung 1d is the next question.** One change from 1b: needs_lost 1 → 6, so burning energy costs what eating pays; still 4, from each 1b brain, 6M, no bound (g1d-fresh-s1, g1d-sc2-s1; seed 934, queued 9 Nov). Once running stops paying, do they slow down to graze?
