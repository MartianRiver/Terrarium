# 0028 The pond nims never learn to stop: no price, rule or pull on the thrust got a stop of 4 s in seven rungs, though a pull keeps the thrust mean near the clip

date: 2026-11-09 · status: hint · seeds: 9,400,000 × 64 (frozen probe, sampled play, one block for every brain); training seeds 822 to 824 · brains: w8-thirsty-s2 (parent); sc1-e0/e3/e12-s2 (2M each), sc2-e0/e3/e12/e24-s2 (2M), sc3-e0/e12-s2 (6M), sc4, sc5, sc6 e0/e12-s2 (6M each), sc7-b01 and sc7-b1-s2 (6M each); contrast c29-from18-s1/s2, frozen

**Question.** 0024 found the night brains' thrust pinned far past the clip. The same holds for the pond nims, and 0027 found they cross the pond at full speed without stopping to drink. River, on watching the pond runs: more water training while they can't stay still "feels pointless". Can a pond nim learn to stop, and which lever teaches it: a cost for moving, an unpinned thrust, a rule that drinking takes stillness, a pull on the thrust mean back inside the clip?

**World and brains.** The pond world of 0027 with the nim starting thirsty (`water.start` 0.3), the parent w8-thirsty-s2 (0027's W8, seed 2). The water world charges nothing for moving; the colony brain's world charges 0.02 a second at full thrust. Each rung changes one thing, from the rung before or from the sc2 brains (sc3 to sc7 are branches of sc2, not a stack), 2M samples for sc1 and sc2 and 6M after:
- sc1, a cost for moving: world `move` 0 → 0.02 and a reward `effort` of 0 / 3 / 12 (full thrust all game ≈ 0 / 0.27 / 1.1 of a game's ~2.5), effort 0 being the continuation in the same world.
- sc2, a fresh thrust head (`[start] fresh = ["thrust"]`, 0024's n50), on each sc1 brain in its own world; sc2-e24 is effort 24 with the reset from sc1-e12.
- sc3, 6M more of sc2-e0 and sc2-e12's recipes.
- sc4, drinking takes stillness: `[water] still` 0.5, a drink falls with speed and is nothing at 0.5 m/s, branch drink-still.
- sc5, `still` 4.0: full speed (2 m/s) drinks half, a stop drinks all.
- sc6, a bound on the thrust mean alone (`[ppo] bound` 0.001 on thrust only, the action-bound patch from 0024's branch), on sc5's world.
- sc7, the same bound at 0.01 (sc7-b01) and 0.1 (sc7-b1), e12 recipe.

**Method.** A frozen probe (`still_probe.py`, 64 games from seed 9,400,000, sampled play as in training) of every brain's last checkpoint, recording at each choice the thrust mean, the chance of a sampled thrust under 0.2 in absolute value, speed, and where the nim is. "Quiet" is speed under 0.1 m/s with a held thrust under 0.2; a spell is four seconds or more of it. The colony brains were run in the colony world at 360 s, the pond brains for 180 s. The brains differ in more than the lever (their parents, seeds, 6M or 2M samples), so each comparison is a first look at one seed a rung.

**Result.** Pond thrust mean is the median of the brain's raw thrust mean at choices inside the pond, near-zero is the chance of a sampled thrust under 0.2, and the three right-hand columns are per game (a nim); the clip is at 1. Pairs are e0 / e12 unless the row says otherwise.

What each rung did, in the order they ran. sc1: nothing; the pond thrust mean is 4.4 to 4.6 in all three and the parent's 4.5, with no quiet time and no spells, pond visits and food in the noise. sc2: the thrust is unpinned and the cost now acts by price (mean 1.36 / 1.19 / 0.98 at effort 0 / 3 / 12, pond speed 1.94 / 1.92 / 1.78 m/s, dwell 1.30 / 1.60 / 1.77 s), and the near-zero chance in the nest by day is 9.5 / 10.1 / 12.1%, but quiet time is only 0.19 to 0.34 s a minute sampled (about 0 drawn), and relearning to move cost deliveries (3.05 → 2.08 / 2.48 / 2.39). sc3: 6M more re-pins it (mean 2.35 / 1.84, speed 2.0, quiet 0.02 to 0.04), though they are better nims than the pinned parent (alive 88 / 95 s against 75, deliveries 3.6 against 3.05). sc4: no nim ever slowed under 0.5 m/s in the pond, so almost none drank (0.25 s of drinking across all 64 games for e12): every game ends at 25.2 s, thirst arriving from a start of 0.3 at 0.012 a second, and the return is about 0. sc5: the mean comes down but stays past the clip (1.77 / 1.26), a stop is still never sampled, drinking runs at half rate (7.00 / 7.42 s a game, up from 5.44 / 6.52 in sc3), alive falls from 88 / 95 s to 63 / 67 and deliveries to 2.4. sc6: in training the bound engaged and lost (the share of choices past the edge rose 82 → 99% for e0 and 47 → 96% for e12 and the bound loss rose all run); pond mean 1.90 / 1.17, drink 14.9 / 8.2 s a game. sc7: the bound holds the mean near the clip (0.68 / 0.98, the share past the edge at the pond 30 / 41% against sc6's 99.8%, in training 47 → 39% and 47 → 45%), and the near-zero chance is 12 / 7% against 4%, but quiet is 0.72 / 0.30 s a minute, no spell of 4 s anywhere and none at home, drink per game 2.5 / 4.3 s (sc6's 8.2) and alive 48 / 60 s (66).

**What it rules out.** That a price on moving can teach a pinned thrust (sc1): at a mean of 4.5 with a spread of 0.15, every draw clips to full, so every choice pays the same and the gradient sees no difference. That unpinning and a price is enough to hold the thrust down (sc2, sc3): 6M more and PPO drives the mean back past the clip, because in this world going fast pays (a walk-through drink, nothing for a stop), and a price only slows it. That a rule making drinking need a stop is enough on its own (sc4, sc5): taking away the walk-through drink before they can find the new one leaves nothing to learn from, and a gentler slope only makes them drink slower and die sooner. That the weakest bound (0.001) can hold a mean near the clip against a policy that wants to go faster (sc6). What does work, mechanically, is the bound at 0.01, about the right weight (0.001 lost, 1.0 slowed everything in 0024): the mean stays near the clip, the near-zero chance is 12 / 7% and the share past the edge is 30 / 41%. They then slow down outside rather than stop in the pond, and they drink less. Since sc2's fresh head the thrust spread is about 0.6 (0.60 / 0.59 in sc7), so a low draw comes up on 12 / 7% of choices at the pond, but a stop of 4 s takes dozens of low draws in a row, which chance never strings together unless the mean itself falls near 0 there; and nothing in the world asks for stillness except a pond crossed in about 2 s.

It also rules out that stopping is beyond the brain or the action: c29 stops for about a third of every living minute.

**Limits.** Every rung kept the reward at needs 6 against needs_lost 1 with no effort cost (W7c's recipe), which a review after the ladder found pays for turnover: burning energy and water and eating and drinking it back nets about 5× what it cost, so the reward itself may pay for speed. The pond twin of grazing rung 1d (needs_lost 6) tests that. One seed a rung, one block of 64 games, last checkpoints only; every rung is a hint. The rungs differ in sample count (2M for sc1 and sc2, 6M after), so the 6M ones also had more training. The c29 colony brain is in a different world (warmth, mates, 360 s, a longer gamma) and reached stillness mostly at home at night, so what teaches it there may be the cold and the nest rather than the price of moving; frozen, it doesn't say which. sc4 was a bad design, caught only after running (the rule has to leave the old way partly paying); sc1 should have been caught before running too. Not tried: a bound at 0.01 with a longer run or a world where something asks for stillness in the pond (a longer drink at a stop, grazing, where the food itself takes standing still), a narrower thrust spread once the mean is low (with a spread of 0.6, a run of low draws is what's missing), a learned rest action as its own output, a longer horizon.
