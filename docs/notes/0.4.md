# 0.4 Elderflower: notes

What we tried on the way to growth and memory, what the numbers said, and what changed because of them. Kept as we go, so the dead ends stay in. Scores are from `terrarium.evals` (64 episodes, seed 1,000,000) unless a line says otherwise; "the 0.3 brain" is `c1c16fc46934` (pbt-y1-28).

## Memory, built

The 0.3 brain decides from what it sees this moment and nothing else. It can't know where a flower opened a minute ago, or that the food it was heading for went behind a block, and most of the flowers it found were luck. Memory goes in first in 0.4, on its own, so whatever it changes can be pinned on it.

Options we looked at: stacking the last few looks into the input (cheap, but only remembers as far back as the stack), senses made by hand for what to remember (a rule deciding what matters, which the mind isn't allowed), a GRU or an LSTM (notes carried from choice to choice, with learned gates for what to keep), attention over past moments (a transformer over time: strong, but its cost grows with how far back it looks, and PPO with it is harder to get right), and newer state-space models. A GRU is the plainest of the ones that learn what to keep, has fewer weights than an LSTM for the same notes, and has the most PPO practice behind it (CleanRL's recurrent PPO is the same shape).

How it went in ([brain.md](../brain.md), "Memory"):

- The GRU reads the self token's output each choice, and a link reads its notes back in before the heads. The link starts at zero, so a brain given memory acts exactly as before. Checked on the 0.3 brain given 128 notes: its scores in a 4-episode, 60 s colony eval came out identical to the decimal (59.6 s alive both), and its weights went from 270,349 to 385,933.
- PPO for a brain with memory replays each creature's rollout in order, from the notes it had when the rollout began. A test checks that every living choice gets the same value and log odds in the replay as it had while collecting, through deaths and episodes ending inside the rollout, and that the same test fails if the notes aren't wiped where they were.
- `evals --wiped` blanks the notes before every choice: the check that a gain comes from remembering and not from the extra weights.

## A world that needs memory: flower spots

A test of memory needs something seen that isn't in view later and is worth coming back to. In colony.toml none of that holds for flowers: they open anywhere, so there's nothing to remember, and food smells, which already leads the way to it without remembering anything. If places were the same in every episode, a brain could learn them into its weights from the home sense alone (how far and which way home is), so that wouldn't need memory either. They have to be new every episode and stay put within it.

`configs/spots.toml`: colony.toml with one colony of six (room to hatch to eight), no nights, 180 s episodes, 5 food instead of 22, and flowers every 8–16 s that open only at two spots drawn anew each episode, at least 12 m apart, so from one you can't see the other (rays reach 8 m). Flowers don't smell.

Before any training, the scripted policies and the 0.3 brain there:

| | seconds alive | food a minute | flowers each | died | Terrikins at the end | hatched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| greedy | 82.7 | 2.13 | 0.13 | 299 | 1.33 | 0.00 |
| carrier | 100.3 | 2.69 | 0.22 | 275 | 1.70 | 0.00 |
| chaser | 134.6 | 2.56 | 1.65 | 153 | 3.66 | 0.05 |
| spotter | 145.8 | 1.92 | 1.51 | 117 | 4.55 | 0.38 |
| the 0.3 brain | 152.8 | 4.18 | 0.28 | 141 | 5.28 | 1.48 |

Per colony a run; Terrikins at the end out of up to 8, starting from 6.

- Flowers are most of what keeps a scripted colony alive here: chaser, carrier that runs for any flower it sees, lives a third longer than carrier and loses no colonies.
- Knowing the spots is worth something even to a script: spotter ends with 0.9 more Terrikins than chaser and 36 fewer deaths. Part of that may be waiting still at a spot (cheap) rather than wandering, not only knowing where to wait.
- The 0.3 brain beats both without eating many flowers (0.28 each against chaser's 1.65): it forages food far better than the scripts and hatches from its store. Flowers are where it has room to gain, and finding them again is what memory is for.

## The first memory runs: memory unused

From the 0.3 brain in spots.toml, 8M samples each, two seeds, with 128 notes or none, its own train settings (repeat 2, steps 128, 32 arenas, lr 5e-5, gamma 0.999). Side by side on one rented RTX 3070, scored there on the GPU, 64 episodes each:

| | Terrikins at the end | flowers each | seconds alive | died |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the 0.3 brain | 5.27 ± 0.17 | 0.28 | 152.9 | 141 |
| no memory, seed 1 | 5.61 ± 0.19 | 0.33 | 156.1 | 160 |
| no memory, seed 2 | 5.58 ± 0.16 | 0.31 | 156.0 | 141 |
| 128 notes, seed 1 | 5.64 ± 0.15 | 0.33 | 158.9 | 144 |
| the same, notes wiped | 5.58 ± 0.16 | 0.31 | 160.0 | 135 |
| 128 notes, seed 2 | 5.66 ± 0.16 | 0.35 | 155.1 | 152 |
| the same, notes wiped | 5.61 ± 0.14 | 0.32 | 159.5 | 129 |

Spotter, for scale, ends with 4.55 and eats 1.51 flowers each.

- Training in spots helps a little either way: a third of a Terrikin more at the end, with or without memory.
- Memory adds nothing yet. With notes, the brains score the same as without, and wiping the notes before every choice changes nothing beyond the noise. They aren't using them.
- None of them went for flowers: about 0.3 each, where chaser eats 1.65. A brain that doesn't value flowers has nothing to remember about them, so this world didn't put memory to the test.
- The memory update is 5.3 times slower (7.5 s against 1.4 s a 32k-sample round on the 3070). Side by side, 3,300 samples a second against 7,600.

A flaw both arms shared: every arena starts and ends its episode together (`[start] staggered` only moves the time of day), so each update sees one moment of an episode, all the start or all the end. The night worlds have it too: staggering only spread the time of day, not where each arena is in its episode. The fix being tried: cut each arena's first episode short by a random amount, so they fall out of step from the start (`[start] spread`).

Rich spots (`configs/spots-rich.toml`): spots.toml with 3 berries instead of 5 and each flower worth 8 food, so the spots pay far more. Before training there (Mac, CPU): chaser 1.95 at the end, spotter 2.89 (1.53 flowers each), the 0.3 brain 2.62 (0.21 flowers).

- Still nothing from memory: in every world, wiping the notes changes the score by less than its error.
- In rich spots both brains pass spotter without going for flowers (0.22 each, against spotter's 1.53), even with a flower worth eight berries. Training never finds the flowers worth chasing, so there's nothing for memory to keep.
- A learning rate four times higher is the best spots brain yet, a whole Terrikin over the 0.3 brain, but it gets there with or without its notes.

What these worlds ask to be remembered pays off later than that: a flower spot comes round again every 8–16 s, and the patch matters again only after a trip home. The GRU can only learn to keep what pays within the window; longer than that, it gets no credit for it. Steps 256 (25.6 s) on its own didn't change that in spots.
- Next, a world where what's worth remembering pays inside the window: the patch, with short cold nights that take its berries and grow them back in the same place at dawn (`patch-dusk.toml`: 12 s of night in every 40, steps 256).

## The patch at dusk, and why the notes go unread

`configs/patch-dusk.toml`: one patch, with cold nights of 12 s in every 40 that take its berries and grow them back in the same place at dawn, 240 s episodes. A Terrikin that went home for the night and remembers where the patch was can walk straight back; one that doesn't has to search again. Before training (Mac, CPU, 64 episodes): greedy and carrier lose every colony (38 and 40 s alive), nester loses 42 of 64 (0.75 Terrikins at the end), the 0.3 brain 15 of 64 (2.20 at the end, 124 s alive).

16M samples each, steps 256 (25.6 s, so the night fits in what training reaches back over), scored on the GPU:

| | Terrikins at the end | seconds alive | colonies lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| the 0.3 brain | 2.30 ± 0.33 | 119.7 | 23 |
| no memory | 2.59 ± 0.34 | 127.8 | 15 |
| 128 notes | 2.17 ± 0.34 | 115.9 | 28 |
| the same, notes wiped | 2.33 ± 0.33 | 122.4 | 24 |

Memory unused again, and the memory brain learned less. So we looked inside: the link that reads the notes back starts at zero, and after 8–16M samples its weights average 0.003, against 0.13 for the brain's other weights, in every memory brain so far. At that size what the notes say barely reaches the heads, so there's nothing for training to improve them by. The link was meant to start at zero and grow once the notes were useful; at a learning rate of 5e-5 it hardly grows at all.

The fix being tried: the memory (the GRU and the link) learns 10–20 times faster than the rest of the brain (`[ppo] memory_lr`). On the Mac it got the link as big in 262k samples as 16M had before.

With the memory learning faster, 64 episodes on the GPU:

| | Terrikins at the end | the same, notes wiped | link weights |
|---|---|---|---|
| patch at dusk, 16M, memory 20× | 2.56 ± 0.32 | 2.53 ± 0.30 | 0.009 |
| spots, 8M at lr 2e-4, memory 10× | 4.12 ± 0.11 | 5.53 ± 0.14 | 0.016 |

- In spots, for the first time, wiping the notes changes the score, by more than a whole Terrikin. But the wrong way: the brain does better without its notes. The link grew five times bigger, so the notes now reach the heads, but what they carry is noise to it, and it hasn't learned to use them in 8M.
- That's still a step: the memory is connected now. Next is a gentler multiple (3–5×), more samples, or a link that starts small but not at zero.

Then all three, at 16M samples and lr 2e-4 in spots (64 episodes, GPU, except the second no-memory seed, on the Mac's CPU):

| spots, 16M | Terrikins at the end | notes wiped | hatched each colony | link weights |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no memory, seed 1 | 7.45 ± 0.05 | | 2.17 | |
| no memory, seed 2 | 7.33 | | 2.88 | |
| memory | 7.48 | 7.47 | 2.84 | 0.005 |
| memory 3× | 4.38 | 4.25 | 0 | |
| memory 5× | 4.42 | 6.30 | 0 | |
| link starting small and random (0.016) | 6.16 | 5.92 | 2.38 | 0.017 |
| the same, memory 3× | 3.03 | 3.08 | 0 | |
| patch at dusk, memory 5× | 2.77 | 2.86 | 0.88 | |

- At 1× the memory is still unused after 16M, and a random start barely moves (0.016 to 0.017) while costing a Terrikin.
- Faster memory breaks the brain: at 3× and 5× they stop storing and hatching altogether. The fast part pulls on the whole brain, since what it reads back goes straight into the trunk.
- Two seeds without memory land 0.1 apart, so that's about the noise between runs.
- The bigger lesson is the world. In spots the brain without memory ends with 7.45 Terrikins, far above the scripted spotter that remembers where flowers opened (4.55). It eats 0.3 flowers each; berries carry it. Remembering the spots is worth little next to everything else it can do, so there's nothing pulling the memory into use.

## A flash: a world where only memory pays

Memory gets its own small test, as a maze would be: `configs/flash.toml` is spots with only 2 berries, and flowers that open every 3–6 s at one of two spots 10 m apart, seen only for the first second (`[flowers] seen = 1.0`) and then lying unseen, still there to eat, until they close at 8 s. To eat one you have to remember where it flashed. 32 episodes, Mac CPU:

| | Terrikins at the end | colonies lost | flowers eaten of those opened |
|---|---|---|---|
| chaser (goes for flowers it can see, no memory) | 1.06 | 14 of 32 | 24% |
| spotter (remembers where flowers opened) | 5.38 | 0 | (the counter counts some twice) |
| the 0.3 brain, untrained | 2.34 | 0 | 8% |

Memory is worth about four Terrikins here. A brain that can only turn towards a flash and keep walking has its body for a memory, which is what the chaser does, and it starves. Training: no memory vs 128 notes, a second seed, and a 25.6 s window (steps 256).

### The first flash runs: memory still unread

16M samples each from the 0.3 brain in flash.toml (lr 2e-4, memory at 1×), two runs to a pod. All scored on the Mac's CPU, 64 episodes:

| | Terrikins at the end | with notes wiped | stored each |
|---|---|---|---|
| chaser | 0.73 | | 1.7 |
| spotter | 5.33 | | 5.4 |
| the 0.3 brain, untrained | 2.12 | | 1.1 |
| 16M, no memory | 3.12 | | 1.3 |
| 16M, 128 notes | 2.47 | 2.50 | 0.0 |
| 16M, 128 notes, seed 2 | 2.23 | 2.09 | 1.2 |
| 16M, 128 notes, steps 256 | 3.20 | 3.17 | 0.9 |

- None of the memory brains beats the one without, and wiping their notes makes no difference, so whatever they've written down goes unread. The longer window lands level with no memory, which is what more samples per update would do on its own.
- Seed 1 with memory stopped storing altogether, as the faster memory did in spots. Runs vary a lot here: the two seeds with memory are 0.24 apart, and no-memory seeds in spots were 0.1 apart.
- So a world where only memory pays isn't enough. The link that reads the notes back starts at zero, so the reward has nothing to push the notes with, and in 16M it never finds one.

What's being tried next: a side task for the memory (its notes also learn to say where the last flower it saw open is, a supervised guess trained alongside PPO, so the notes fill with something useful before the brain learns to read them), and 32M more of the 128-note brain against 32M more without, to see if it's only time.

### A side task for the notes: they remember, the brain doesn't look

The side task (`[ppo] hint`): a small head reads the notes and guesses where the newest flower it saw is, in its own frame, for as long as that flower is open, and the error of that guess is added to the loss at weight 1 or 0.2. It trains the memory directly, whatever the reward does. 16M each from the 0.3 brain in flash.toml, both on one RTX 3060 pod (91 min, A$0.21 (US$0.146) at 1.424, ECB 2026-10-23), scored on the Mac like the runs above:

| | Terrikins at the end | with notes wiped | seconds alive | guess error at the end (guessing nothing) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16M, no memory | 3.12 | | 114.2 | |
| 16M, 128 notes, side task 1 | 1.94 | 2.00 | 85.5 | 0.29 (0.63) |
| 16M, 128 notes, side task 0.2 | 1.84 | 1.88 | 80.7 | 0.43 (0.52) |

- The notes learned it: at weight 1 the guess is about half as wrong as guessing nothing, and still improving at 16M. So the GRU can hold where a flower flashed for the seconds it's needed.
- The brain never reads it: wiping the notes changes nothing, flowers eaten stay at 3–4% of those opened (spotter eats most), and both runs end worse than without memory and worse than the untrained 0.3 brain (2.12). They head for flowers in view more often (21–29% against 11%), but that's sight, not memory.
- The side task costs: its gradient runs through the same body as the policy, and both runs stored nothing and lived 30 s less than the run without memory.
- Verdict: a dead end as built, with one thing learned. Filling the notes isn't the problem any more; reading them is. The link from the notes back into the brain (`recall`) starts at zero and only PPO moves it, and in 16M it doesn't.

More time without memory doesn't help either: 32M more of the brain with none (48M in all, on the Mac) ends with 2.92 Terrikins and 117.4 s alive, against 3.12 and 114.2 at 16M.

Nor with it. The 128-note brain got 24M more on a lent laptop (three 8M jobs through the queue), and ended worse: 1.50 Terrikins and 63.8 s alive at 40M (1.27 wiped), against 2.47 at 16M. Its training episodes held at about 114 s through the first two jobs and fell to 80 s in the last quarter of the third, so the brain fell apart late, and the evals caught it after. Reading only the last line of a run's metrics had made this look like an eval bug: a line covers a handful of episodes (7 there, at 140 s), so a run's episode length has to be averaged over many lines.

Something every flash run shares, found reading their metrics: the first update moves the brain far more than any after it. The KL between the brain before and after an update is 8.1 on the first one without memory, 29–44 with it, and 0.2–0.9 by the fourth. A fresh Adam optimizer takes steps of about the learning rate in every weight at once, whatever its gradient says, so the first update shoves the whole 0.3 brain, and memory runs have more fresh weights to shove with. Some of what the parent knew is thrown away before training has a say. The usual fixes are a learning rate that ramps up over the first few updates, or stopping an update's epochs early once its KL passes a limit.

Options from here: hand the brain the side task's own guess as a sense (where it thinks the flower is, two numbers, learned from its own memory, so nothing is scripted), which is a far easier thing for PPO to use than 128 notes; start `recall` small but not zero; or stop the side task's gradient at the notes so it can't pull on the rest of the brain.

### A flower sense: told the answer, it still doesn't go

The first option, with a control that settles more than it does. A new sense, three numbers: where the last flower it saw open is, in its own frame, and whether it's still open. Two ways to fill it:

- **told:** the truth, from the world. That's what perfect memory would give, so it says what remembering is worth here at all. No notes.
- **guess:** the side task's own guess from the notes, made before each choice. Its weights are trained only by the side task, and the brain reads the guess like any other sense.

Both start from the 0.3 brain, whose weights for the new sense start at zero like any new input, 16M in flash.toml on one RTX 3070 pod (80 min). Scored on the pod's GPU, so no-memory is shown as the pod scored it (3.06; 3.12 on the Mac):

| | Terrikins at the end | sense wiped | flowers eaten of those opened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16M, no memory | 3.06 | | |
| told | 2.30 | 2.14 | 5% |
| guess (side task error 0.35 against 0.63 for guessing nothing) | 1.86 | 1.83 | 3% |

- Told where the flower is, it still eats 1 flower in 20, and ends below the brain without the sense. The weights reading the new sense grew to a tenth the size of the others in 16M (0.011 against 0.12).
- So the memory runs were testing the wrong thing. Remembering where a flower is can't help a brain that doesn't go to flowers, and this one doesn't: it never learned to, in spots or here.

### Can it learn to go to flowers at all?

Flowers seen the whole time they're open (`configs/flash-seen.toml`, flash with `seen = 0`), so nothing needs remembering. 64 episodes (baselines on the Mac, runs on the pod's GPU; one RTX 4060 Ti with 32 cores ran each at 10,500 samples a second, three times the 3070):

| | Terrikins at the end | flowers eaten of those opened | heads for a flower in view |
|---|---|---|---|
| the 0.3 brain, untrained | 2.19 | 7% | 18% |
| chaser (walks to any flower it sees) | 3.86 | 64% | 67% |
| spotter (knows the spots) | 5.64 | most | 85% |
| 16M | 3.64 | 27% | 20% |
| 16M, learning rate ramped up over the first 10 updates | 3.38 | 17% | 20% |

- It does learn some flowers it can see: 27% eaten, and most of the way to the chaser at the end. It still doesn't head for one in view any more often than before, so it's finding them some other way: more likely by hanging about where they keep opening than by going to one it sees.
- The ramped learning rate takes the first update's KL from 32 to 0.02, but ends a little lower here, within what seeds differ by.

Then time. 32M more of the 16M run (48M in all), and a second seed of the 16M run, on the same 32-core pod (51 min for both):

| | Terrikins at the end | seconds alive | flowers eaten of those opened | heads for a flower in view | hatched each colony |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16M, seed 2 | 3.39 | | 24% | 17% | |
| **48M** | **6.64** | **164.5** | **69%** | **40%** | 2.34 |

- It gets there. Between 16M and 48M it learned flowers: it eats more of them than the chaser, heads for twice as many as at 16M, and ends with more Terrikins than either scripted policy, the spotter included, hatching in 58 of 64 episodes.
- The two 16M seeds are 0.25 apart, so the ramped learning rate's 3.38 is within seed noise of no ramp.
- This explains the memory runs better than anything about memory does. Going to flowers takes this brain about 48M to learn even when it can see them, and every memory run had 16M from a brain that never had. Memory can only help a skill the brain already has.
- So the next memory test starts here: the 48M flower brain in flash, where flowers show for a second, with 128 notes against none.

The ramp in flash too, with and without memory, 16M each from the 0.3 brain, scored on the pod's GPU:

| | Terrikins at the end | with notes wiped | seconds alive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16M, no memory, ramped | 3.08 ± 0.11 | | 123.0 |
| 16M, 128 notes, ramped | 2.48 ± 0.08 | 2.41 ± 0.09 | 103.3 |

Both land where the runs without it did (3.12 and 2.47), and wiping the notes still changes nothing. Whatever the first update throws away, training gets back within 16M, so the shove isn't why memory goes unread. The ramp was meant to stay on for memory runs, since it costs nothing, but it lived only in the scratch trees these runs trained from: a review on 30 October found it had never reached the code, and it went in as `[ppo] warmup` (updates to ramp over, 0 off).

### Memory on the brain that goes to flowers

The test the last section set up: the 48M flower brain (from flash-seen) in flash, where a flower shows for a second, 16M more with 128 notes against none, seed 1 each, ramped, on the 32-core pod (58 min for both). Scored on the pod's GPU:

| | Terrikins at the end | with notes wiped | seconds alive | flowers eaten of those opened | stored each |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the 48M flower brain, untrained here | 3.67 ± 0.18 | | 132.0 | 16% | 3.0 |

### Fresh brains: memory from the first tick

Every memory run so far forked a brain that had learned a whole other world without memory. These two start from nothing (width 128, coins, seed 7), one with 128 notes from the first tick and one without, 32M each in flash on one pod (112 min for both). Scored on the pod's GPU:

| | Terrikins at the end | seconds alive | food each | stored each | died (of 512) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a fork of the 0.3 brain, 16M, no memory (above) | 3.08 | 123.0 | 5.81 | 1.13 | 197 |
| fresh, no memory | 4.38 ± 0.08 | 150.1 | 5.87 | 0 | 104 |
| fresh, 128 notes | 4.23 ± 0.08 | 149.0 | 5.43 | 0 | 113 |
| fresh, 128 notes, wiped before every choice | 3.64 ± 0.09 | 131.6 | 4.81 | 0 | 151 |
| fresh, no memory, seed 2 | 3.98 | 142.5 | 5.60 | 0 | 129 |
| fresh, 128 notes, seed 2 | 4.73 | 159.2 | 5.68 | 0 | 81 |
| fresh, 128 notes, seed 2, wiped before every choice | 3.94 | 146.3 | 5.24 | 0 | 132 |

- **The first brain that reads its notes, but only from the last moment.** Wiping them before every choice costs 0.59 Terrikins, 17 s alive and a tenth of its food, about five times the error; in every fork the same wipe changed nothing. Wiping them less often shows how far back that goes (64 episodes on the Mac's CPU, where its notes kept score 4.33):

| notes wiped | Terrikins at the end | seconds alive |
|---|---|---|
| never | 4.33 ± 0.09 | 151.7 |
| before every choice (0.1 s) | 3.55 ± 0.09 | 129.2 |
| every second | 4.59 ± 0.08 | 155.5 |
| every 5 s | 4.39 ± 0.07 | 152.1 |
| every 20 s | 4.33 ± 0.07 | 150.8 |
| every minute | 4.31 ± 0.08 | 150.8 |

- A wipe once a second costs nothing, so what it reads from its notes is gone within a second: what it was just doing, a turn in progress, not where anything is. It's a memory used as momentum. Wiping before every choice hurts because it loses even that, or because blank notes every tick are something it never sees in training.
- It doesn't use them for flowers either: it eats 3% of those opened, the brain without memory 4%.
- It leans on its notes but isn't clearly better for them: 4.23 against 4.38 without in the first pair, 4.73 against 3.98 in a second pair (new brains from torch seed 8, run seed 2; errors about ±0.08 as in the first). Averaged over both, memory is ahead by 0.3, but one pair says no and one says yes, so two seeds can't settle it.
- The second pair reads its notes too: wiped before every choice it loses 0.79 Terrikins and 13 s alive, so a brain trained with memory from the start using its notes holds in both seeds.
- A fresh brain beats every fork here, with or without memory, and the difference is what it does with food. Both find about the same, but the fork sends a fifth of it into the store, as the 0.3 brain learned to, and starves beside it: 106 of its 197 deaths have jelly at home, a store that brain almost never eats from. The fresh brains never store at all. The habit from the colony world holds even when it kills them, and 16M (or 48M) in flash doesn't train it out.
- So memory from the first tick gets the notes used, which forking never did, but for the shortest thing they can hold. Remembering a place takes something that pays for holding it longer than a second.

### Flowers that show for less and less

The fresh brains use their notes only for the last second, so the notes need something that pays for holding a place longer. A curriculum, from the 48M flower brain (it goes to flowers it can see): flowers seen for 3 s (8M), then 2 s (8M), then 1 s, which is flash (16M), ramped, 128 notes against none, two seeds, each stage scored in flash on the pod's GPU (64 episodes):

| | Terrikins at the end, after 3 s | after 2 s | after flash | notes wiped, after flash | flowers eaten, after flash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Flash itself pulls every run down: after its 16M both seeds end where they started or lower, memory or not (3.42–3.89), and seed 2's memory brain falls from 4.62 to 3.48. ### More time for the fresh brains: worse, and memory falls apart

The fresh pair (seed 1, 32M each) got 48M more in flash, ramped, on one RTX 3080 pod; the memory brain was stopped at 40M of its 48M to stay in the night's budget and scored from its last checkpoint. All on the Mac's CPU, 64 episodes:

| | Terrikins at the end | notes wiped | seconds alive |
|---|---|---|---|
| fresh, no memory, 32M | 4.48 ± 0.08 | | 152.3 |
| then 48M more | 3.92 ± 0.07 | | 144.8 |
| fresh, 128 notes, 32M | 4.33 ± 0.09 | 3.55 | 151.7 |
| then 40M more | 2.56 ± 0.07 | 3.50 | 106.5 |

- More time made both worse. The memory brain fell apart: training episodes slid from 137 s at 8M to 106 s at 40M, and now its notes hurt it (wiped, it does better). That's the second memory brain to fall apart late in flash, after the one on the lent laptop.
- Both runs show the same drift through training: the policy's entropy falls the whole way (−1.3 to −2.2 with memory, −1.4 to −2.8 without), and with memory the change per update (KL) climbs from 0.025 to 0.055. With the learning rate held at 2e-4 and no annealing, the actions keep narrowing and each update moves a narrower policy further. The usual fixes: anneal the learning rate on long runs, a bigger entropy bonus, or stop an update's epochs once its KL passes a limit.

### Ramping the learning rate down: no more falling apart, and no more notes

If the late falls come from updates that stay as big as they started while the policy narrows, annealing the learning rate to nothing over the run should stop them. Annealed runs from the same fresh brains in flash (scored on the Mac's CPU, 64 episodes):

|---|---|---|---|
| fresh, no memory, 32M | 152.3 | 4.48 | |

So what goes wrong late for memory brains in flash isn't only the size of the updates, and flash isn't a clean test of memory: there's always food to find without remembering anything. That's what peekaboo is for (below).

## Droppings: picked up, and put straight back down

Something to give carrying a use: meals make droppings. Every 3 meals (berries, jelly or flowers), 15 s later, a dropping lands behind whoever ate. It lies for 180 s and makes anyone within 0.6 m of it a little sick (0.002 energy a second for each, a fifth of what being alive costs). A dropping can be carried like a berry, and one being carried makes nobody sick. Terrikins see droppings on their rays, and feel how dirty the ground around them is. Nothing pays for moving one: the only reason to is the sickness. In colony.toml with droppings, from the colony brain that learned to carry (above), 32M samples, scored on the GPU, 64 episodes:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| without droppings, untrained (16 episodes) | 6.31 | 320.5 | 0 | | |
| with droppings, untrained (16 episodes) | 3.96 | 246.2 | 4 of 16 | | |

- Carrying makes it worse. The brains that can't carry droppings end with more Terrikins than the ones that can.
- Picking up the dropping beside you stops it making you sick that tick, so that pays at once. Where it gets put down only costs later, and mostly someone else.

## Calls: nobody listens yet

A side experiment, not on the roadmap until 0.6: could Terrikins invent a call? A new yes/no action `call` lights the signal bulb the viewer has always drawn, and every nestmate within 20 m hears the nearest call, as how loud (how near) and which way it came from. Calling costs 0.002 energy a second. The world is one patch (above), where the patch can't be seen or smelled from afar, so a Terrikin that has found it could bring the others by calling. Nothing pays for calling except what the colony gets from it (half of each reward is the colony's average). Scored with the calls heard, and deaf (nothing heard), 64 episodes:

| | Terrikins at the end | deaf | calls with food in view | with none |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the 0.3 brain, never trained with calls | 3.69 ± 0.40 | 3.69 ± 0.40 | 5.0% | 5.0% |
| 32M, seed 2 (on the Mac, CPU) | 4.42 ± 0.42 | 4.59 ± 0.41 | 8.2% | 7.1% |
| 32M, seed 3 | 2.92 ± 0.42 | 3.36 ± 0.40 | 4.2% | 3.6% |
| 32M with 128 notes (wiped: 4.14) | 4.11 ± 0.42 | 4.03 ± 0.41 | 15.3% | 14.8% |
| 32M, all of each reward shared over the colony | 3.28 ± 0.36 | 3.48 ± 0.39 | 7.6% | 6.5% |
| 32M, twice the berries in the patch | 5.67 ± 0.39 | 5.88 ± 0.38 | 3.3% | 3.2% |

- No meaning: they call about as often with food in view as without, and deafening them changes nothing (if anything they do a little better deaf, within the noise). Nor do they turn to a call: while hearing one with no food in view, how squarely they face it averages 0.06 (1 is straight at it, 0 is chance), the same as the untrained brain's 0.03.
- This is the chicken and egg of a signal: calling only pays once others come to calls, and coming to calls only pays once calls mean something. Starting from noise at 5% a tick, 32M samples didn't break it.
- What could: a cue that already means something, given by the world, not the brain (eating makes a sound nestmates can hear, so listening pays from the start, and a call can grow out of it), or a reward for changing what others do (social influence, Jaques et al. 2019).

## Slack: a body that lasts twice as long

The idea behind "room to try things" below: a Terrikin's body is about 30 s from starving, so trying anything new mostly ends in starving before it pays, and PPO learns not to try. `configs/colony-slack.toml` halves every energy cost and every food's energy, so a full body lasts about 60 s while a minute of life still needs the same food. Two forks of the 0.3 brain, 16M each on one pod (50 min), one in colony.toml and one in the slack world, each scored in both worlds beside the 0.3 brain untrained (64 episodes, GPU):

| | colony.toml: Terrikins at the end | seconds alive | berries picked up each | colony-slack.toml: Terrikins at the end | seconds alive | berries picked up each |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the 0.3 brain | 6.30 | 324.5 | 3.14 | 6.63 | 348.3 | 2.65 |
| 16M in colony.toml | 6.24 | 317.7 | 4.63 | 6.64 | 342.3 | 3.79 |
| 16M in the slack world | 6.28 | 320.8 | 2.75 | 6.66 | 348.2 | 2.31 |

- Slack changed nothing that shows. The 0.3 brain, untrained, already lives as long in the slack world (348 s, every colony to the end, half the deaths) as the brain trained there, and that brain carries no more berries than it did. The fork in the normal world is the one that carries more, as the carry runs above did, without being better for it.
- So slack on its own isn't what was missing, at least for a brain that already knows its world: with nothing to gain from a new action, a cheaper try still isn't worth anything. Carrying pays nothing in either world. The test that matters puts slack and a payoff together: the vault with doors you can carry (half the food behind them), normal against slack.
- 16M more in colony.toml doesn't move the 0.3 brain either (6.24 against 6.30): it has levelled off there.

## Young: a childhood

The forks that went nowhere above all have the same thing in common: trying something new costs a body that's never far from starving. Animals that learn the most get a protected childhood first, fed while they learn. `[young]` gives hatchlings one (`configs/colony-young.toml`: colony.toml with a young minute): for their first 60 s they walk at 70% of top speed, everything costs them half, cold included, and in their own nest they eat from the store whenever they aren't full, not only below the hunger line. They can sense how young they are (schema v10), and the viewer draws them smaller. With it off, nothing changes: the 0.3 brain scores the same in colony.toml to the last digit over 8 episodes.

As first built, the young took a whole jelly a bite, and their bodies are as big as a grown one's, so a hatchling near full ate a jelly and threw most of it away. One episode of the 0.3 brain in 8 arenas:

## A nursery: doors, with starving taken away

## Kids' games in the nursery

If a nursery can teach carrying doors, it can teach other things, and the games children play are a good list of them: tag is chasing and getting away, hide-and-seek is using what's in the way, capture the flag is going somewhere on purpose and getting back, and peekaboo is remembering what you can't see. Each is a small world where nobody can starve (the babies are young the whole game, so living costs them a hundredth of what it costs a grown one, and there's no food to look for), the points sum to nothing between the sides, and one brain plays every side, told which by a sense. Each game is a section that's off in every other world (`[tag]`, `[seek]`, `[flag]`, `[peek]`), so nothing else changes; schema v11 adds what the games need to see (SELF `it`, `seeker`, `waiting`, the way to the other flag and whether ours is taken; RAY `it`, `carrier`).

- **Tag** (`configs/nursery-tag.toml`): five babies, two pillars, a minute. Touching another passes on being it, with 2 s before it can be passed back; being it costs a point a second, shared out among the rest.
- **Hide-and-seek** (`nursery-seek.toml`): two colonies of two, the seekers frozen for the first 15 s, eight light boxes to carry and a wall in the middle. Every second a seeker's rays fall on a hider pays the seekers and costs the hiders, and the other way round.
- **Capture the flag** (`nursery-flag.toml`): two colonies of three in two walled bases at either end with a doorway each, rocks between and a canyon down the sides, a little like a well-known shooter's map. Walk into their base to take their flag, home to capture it; a touch on the carrier sends it back.
- **Peekaboo** (`nursery-peek.toml`, `nursery-peek-spin.toml`): one baby, two pillars, 90 s. A berry turns up 2.5–6 m in front of it, on one of its rays, for a second, then goes out of sight but stays. Finding it pays a point and the next turns up a second later; one not found in 10 s moves. With spin, the baby is turned round at random the moment the berry hides, so walking straight on isn't enough: it has to remember where it was. The berry had to sit on a ray: placed anywhere in view, at 5 m it fell between rays most of the time and was never seen.

## Honest worlds: what a review found

With every run stopped, a fresh read of the code and the results, by a reviewer told to look for what we were getting wrong, turned up three things that made the nursery games easier or harder than they looked. Each was checked by hand before anything changed.

### Rays with a blind spot

16 rays spread evenly over the 200° fan put two either side of dead ahead and none on it, and they're thin lines, so a berry straight ahead slips between them past about 3 m. A probe on the Mac (random walkers, 8 arenas, a look a second for 60 s) counted how often something in front of a Terrikin is reported by some ray, by distance:

| colony.toml | look ms | Terrikins 6–8 m | berries 2–4 m | berries 6–8 m | flowers 6–8 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 thin (before) | 4.2 | 47% | 66% | 33% | 28% |
| 17 thin | 4.6 | 49% | 70% | 35% | 39% |
| 17 at half width | 5.1 | 48% | 81% | 47% | 68% |
| 33 thin | 7.8 | 78% | 85% | 56% | 68% |
| 33 at half width | 10.1 | 75% | 86% | 64% | 78% |

Count matters more than width: a wide ray still reports only the nearest thing in its slice, so far things hide behind near ones. 17 rays at half width (`[vision] cover = 0.5`) cost about the same as 16 and fix the blind spot. 33 see far Terrikins much better (tag: 41% → 76% at 6–8 m), but cost about twice as much in training (rays 12.6 → 31.0 ms a tick for 32 arenas, the update 387 → 842 ms a 4,096-sample batch on the M3), so they wait for a game that needs them. In hide-and-seek the far ones are mostly behind blocks (9% → 16% even with 33), so the blind spot isn't seek's problem. A fovea (rays packed to the middle, shorter at the sides; `spread`, `side`) saw less far out than the same wide rays spread evenly (16 of them: Terrikins at 6–8 m 30% against 46%), so it stays off.

Every nursery game now has 17 rays at half width; the worlds the 0.3 line trained in keep 16, so their brains are scored as they were. Ray tokens carry their own angle, so any brain loads with any fan. `[start] spread` (train config) starts each arena at a random point in its episode, so a game's early and late moments mix in every update, as staggered starts did for nights.

### Drift after the spin

Peekaboo spins the baby round as the berry hides, so finding it means remembering where it was. But the spin turned the body, not the way it was sliding: a policy with no memory that turned to face its own drift and walked on found 43% of berries. The drift turns with the body now (`Physics.turn_velocity`), and the same policy finds 8% (walking straight on 9%, always told 97%). A test keeps it closed. It goes in the holes table below.

### Memory that starts at zero

A brain given memory reads its notes through a link that starts at zero, so it acts exactly as before. The review asked whether that zero also starves the notes of anything to learn from. A toy task (a cue for one step, a wait, then the turn that matches the cue pays), 64 streams, our own brain and recurrent PPO, one seed each:

| wait | zero (as built) | small link (0.1 of a fresh layer) | zero, plus a side job (say what you saw 8 steps ago) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 s | learned by 0.2M samples | | |
| 3 s | learned at 1.8M | 1.5M | 1.1M |
| 6 s | not in 2.5M | 1.6M | 1.1M |
| 10 s | not in 2.5M | not in 2.5M | 1.0M |

So the zero start isn't fatal at short waits, but at peekaboo's waits (up to 10 s) it may be what kept the memory brains level with the ones without. A small link helps up to 6 s; past that only the side job learned, since it pays the notes for keeping the past before anything else does. `train --recall 0.1` starts the link small (the brain then acts a little differently from its parent from the first step) and is in now, being one line; the side job is the next thing to build if peekaboo's memory brains still don't pull ahead.

### Second looks in peekaboo

One look at a berry and a long wait is the hardest way to start learning to remember. `[peek] again` brings a hidden berry back into sight where it is for `flash` seconds every `again` seconds, with a spin each time it hides again, so a baby gets more looks at one spot and each wait is shorter. The flash has to be short, or no memory is needed: two scripts with none, in 32 arenas for 120 s:

| flashes | walk straight, turn to it when seen | turn on the spot until seen, then go to it |
|---|---|---|
| none (one look) | 13% | 0% |
| 1 s every 3 s | 28% | 58% |
| 0.3 s every 3 s | 23% | 8% |
| 0.15 s every 3 s | 18% | 2% |

`nursery-peek-again.toml` uses 0.3 s every 3 s for training. In it random finds 7%, the 0.3 brain 21% and always told 99%, against 9%, 25% and 97% in the one-look game (both with 17 rays), so the extra looks don't hand out berries. Brains trained with second looks are scored in the one-look game.

## Tag with a sprint, and somewhere to hide

Tag so far is five babies round two pillars, and once the it was no faster than anyone, getting away mostly came down to running in the open. Two things should make it a richer game: a sprint with a wait between bursts, so when to go matters on both sides, and enough blocks that the one who's it often can't see who it's after.

- **A sprint** (`nursery-tag-sprint.toml`): the tag nursery with `[sprint]` on, 1.6× top speed for 1.5 s, then 5 s before the next. The babies are young the whole game, so the energy it costs is next to nothing; the wait is the price.
- **More and more blocks**: `nursery-tag-blocks.toml` (14 m, four pillars and four walls) and `nursery-tag-hide.toml` (16 m, sixteen blocks: six pillars round the middle, walls and pillars further out). The rays reach 8 m, so in the 16 m world, behind a wall is out of sight.
- **Blocks that move between games** (`[arena] scatter`): each block lands up to that far from where it's set, turned any way, drawn again if it would overlap another, cross a wall or reach a spawn ring (`tests/test_scatter.py`). With one fixed layout a brain could learn the hiding places by heart instead of learning to hide. Smell's walls and doors that swing shut go by where blocks are set, so scatter refuses worlds with those.
- **Tag only on a bump.** A tag passed on with bodies 10 cm apart, and a Terrikin is drawn a little inside its body (only its arms reach the edge), so in the viewer some tags happened with a gap you could see. The reach is 2 cm now, in every tag world: bodies have to meet.

The scripted chaser in the scorer sprints once it's within 2.5 m, and self-play also counts the share of the time a runner has a block between it and whoever's it.

## A maze nursery

Memory has been hard to show in the worlds so far, and a maze is the classic test of it: the same skill as finding food and getting home (0.5), with nothing else in the way. `nursery-maze.toml`: one baby in a 5×5 maze of 3 m rooms, starting in the middle, with one berry somewhere in it; once it's eaten another grows somewhere else a second or two later, for 90 s. The maze is carved anew every episode (`[arena] maze`, a random depth-first walk, so there's exactly one way between any two rooms), from 16 wooden walls: every maze of 5×5 rooms has (5 − 1)² inner walls, so the same blocks are moved into a new maze each time, and no layout repeats often enough to be learned by heart. Babies here can't starve and are never too full to eat (they start half full and a berry is worth a sliver of energy, while still paying as one food).

Before any training, berries found a minute (32 episodes):

| in the maze | berries a minute |
|---|---|
| random | 0.08 |
| greedy (goes for a berry in sight, turns at walls) | 0.23 |
| keeps a wall on its left, goes for a berry in sight (`wall`, no memory) | 0.60 |
| knows the maze and the berry, shortest way (`mapped`) | **5.56** |

- Between wall-following and knowing the way there's a factor of nine, which is what a brain can win by searching well: not walking a corridor it's already walked, heading for parts of the maze it hasn't seen. That needs memory. A brain that learns only to follow walls without it will sit near 0.6.
- The first step is smaller: `nursery-maze-3.toml`, a 3×3 maze of the same rooms (4 walls), where random finds 0.25 a minute, `wall` 3.50 and `mapped` 11.46 (32 episodes each).
- The first grid, on two 3060s, fresh brains with gamma 0.999 and 512 arenas (a world with one baby an arena gives few samples a step otherwise): 8M samples in the 3×3, then 16M more in the 5×5, two seeds each of no memory, 128 notes (`--recall 0.1`), and 128 notes with a side job that asks the notes "have I been in this room before?" (`[ppo] closure`, loop closure as in Mirowski et al. 2016; the world knows each baby's room, `maze.Visits`, and the brain never sees the answer).
- Memory runs were slow to update: the replay stepped the GRU one choice at a time, a few small GPU calls each, and the update was 66–77% of a memory run's time. It now cuts each creature's rollout at its wipes and sends every piece through one GRU call (`Brain.remember_streams`), with the same notes and gradients: 512 creatures × 128 choices forward and back went 248 → 153 ms on the M3's CPU and 168 → 75 ms on its GPU, and in the grid a memory run's update took about as long as one without (three runs sharing a 3060, so a hint, not a clean comparison). All of these go in [efficiency.md](efficiency.md).

## Capture the flag, 300M from fresh: they learned not to play

300M of self-play in `nursery-flag.toml` with the steal rules (a touch takes the flag off its carrier), scored at five points through the run, 128 episodes: zero pickups in self-play and zero captures against a random team at every one, where random players manage 0.09 pickups a game. The action spread fell all run (entropy 6.4 → −1.6). Flag points are zero-sum (a pickup pays one side and charges the other), so with steal on, picking up a flag mostly hands the other side a robbery and their points; both sides settled on never touching it. A dead end as built, at about US$0.46. Paying a pickup without charging the other side, or training against a frozen or random team before self-play, are the fixes to try.

## Peekaboo in steps: second looks further and further apart

The best peekaboo brain learned with second looks: once hidden, the berry flashes back for 0.3 s every 3 s, and it was scored with one look (`nursery-peek-spin`). That's one step from help to no help. A ladder might teach memory better: flashes every 2 s, then every 4 s, then every 8 s, then never, so each step asks the notes to hold the spot a little longer. Four fresh memory brains (128 notes, `--recall 0.1`), each 16M in four chained 4M steps (lr annealed only in the last), side by side on one RTX A4000: two on the ladder, two on 3 s flashes for all four steps, the same chain in the same world as the best brain's start. Scored with one look, 128 episodes:

|---|---|---|---|
| random | 0.43 | 8% | |
| `last_seen` (perfect memory) | 12.05 | 86% | |

## Tag's blind spot, and hearing

Watching tag among sixteen blocks, the runners often end up circling just behind whoever's it, and it circles after them and never gets there. The rays cover 200° in front, so the 160° behind is out of sight, and a runner tucked in there can't be seen. Measured over 32 games of self-play with the best tag brain so far (the ladder's seed 1), someone is within 2 m of the it and behind it, out of view, 31% of the game: 377 spells, 1.6 s on average, 58 of them over 3 s, the longest 6 s. Two random players manage 11%, and with the scripted it that sees everything, runners from the same brain get behind it only 4% of the time. The replay and brain are kept with the other holes.

The odd part is that the it keeps chasing a runner it can't see, and this brain has no memory. To find what carries the chase, the it's senses were cut one at a time before each choice (set to zero for the it only, runners untouched), counting how often it turns toward a runner that's behind it, out of view:

| it's senses cut | turns toward the hidden runner | tags a minute |
|---|---|---|
| none | 70% | 8.88 |
| its last turn | 69% | |
| its last turn and thrust | 68% | |
| how fast it's sliding sideways | 69% | |
| how fast it's going forward | 61% | 5.34 |
| all four | 56% | 7.34 |

No one sense carries it. The likeliest story is the plainest: the it keeps turning the way it was already turning when the runner slipped out of view, and since the runner circles the same way, that keeps it close. A hint, not a finding: cutting the rays too leaves too few ticks to count, and this only rules single senses out. Probes on the brain's insides would be the next tool.

The fix a living thing would have is hearing. `[hearing] range` (0 off, in every world; 3 m in the six tag worlds): each Terrikin hears the nearest other one within range, all the way round and through blocks, as how loud (1 touching, 0 at the range) and which way, appended to what it senses about itself (schema 14, `heard`, `heard_sin`, `heard_cos`; `terrarium/hearing.py`, `tests/test_hearing.py`). Older brains get zeros there and act exactly as before.

Three seeds with hearing and three without (a deaf control: the same world with range 0), each trained from that brain among sixteen blocks, all scored at 13.9M (the slower pod's hearing seed stopped there) over 64 held-out games. Behind is the share of self-play with someone within 2 m behind the it, out of view (32 games on the ordinary seed, not the held-out one: the probe script doesn't use it); catch is tags a minute as the it against random runners; escape is tags a minute on it as runners against the scripted chaser as it (corrected 2026-11-05: this said catch was against the chaser; the scorer plays catch against random runners, which is why the chaser's own catch, 15.89, is random's escape):

| | behind | against itself | catch ↑ | escape ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | 11% | 2.67 | 2.67 | 15.89 |
| the scripted chaser | | 15.34 | 15.89 | 15.34 |
| the starting brain | 31% | 8.75 | 17.84 | 10.97 |
| deaf, seeds 1 / 2 / 3 | 27% / 17% / 19% | 11.69 / 8.66 / 10.52 | 18.77 / 18.94 / 18.00 | 10.28 / 7.08 / 8.75 |
| hearing, seeds 1 / 2 / 3 | 13% / 8% / 14% | 8.17 / 8.06 / 6.97 | 17.41 / 18.20 / 18.42 | 5.03 / 9.39 / 5.31 |
| mean, deaf | 21% | 10.29 | 18.57 | 8.70 |
| mean, hearing | **12%** | 7.73 | 18.01 | **6.58** |

- Training alone shrinks the blind spot (31% to 21% without hearing); hearing takes it to 12%, and every hearing seed is below every deaf one. Spells over 3 s fall from 58 to 5–14 in 32 games with hearing, 9–35 without.
- What hearing helps most is the runners: two of the three hearing seeds are tagged about half as often as the starting brain by a chaser that sees everything; the third is no better than the deaf seeds. A runner can now hear the it coming up behind it.
- As the it, hearing doesn't help: the deaf brains catch a little more (18.6 against 18.0 a minute, intervals overlapping).
- Fewer tags in self-play with hearing (7.7 against 10.3), since both sides hear each other.
- At 16M, the faster pod's three runs said the same (behind 10% and 8% with hearing, 24% deaf; escape 4.89 and 8.88 against 10.78).

Hearing stays on in the tag worlds. Three seeds a side, so it's a finding for the blind spot and a hint for escaping.

## Rival brains: the huge first step was the step

Two copies of the same hide-and-seek brain, one for the hiders and one for the seekers with seats fixed, started with a KL near 40 on the first update (sep-same, notes above), where a brain against itself starts near 0.02, so those runs were put aside as untrustworthy. Checked on one rollout of the same setup: every log odd logged while playing equals what its own brain gives it again (largest difference 0), and an update at a learning rate of 0 moves neither brain. At the run's 3e-4 with a fresh optimizer, one update moves them to a KL of 10 and 47; at 5e-5, 0.04. The data was right all along. A fresh Adam's first steps move every weight by about the learning rate, which a trained brain can't take, and the seekers' brain, with half the samples, took it worst. Rival runs get `warmup` and `kl_stop` from here, and lineages (a brain for each colony) are unblocked.

The fix, checked on the Mac (sep-same-fix, 2M samples, the same pair with `warmup` 10, `kl_stop` 0.02 and 5e-5): the first update's KL was 0.004 for both brains and it stayed between 0.004 and 0.011 the whole run. The seekers gained 46–48 points a game from update 6 on; the hiders never got better.

## Droppings again: piles, and a tidy nursery

The first droppings (above) were only ever picked up for the moment it stopped the sickness. Two changes for a second try. Droppings now lie in piles: each one's sickness is shared out among the droppings within `pile` of it, so a heap is only as bad as one dropping, and carrying them all to one place is worth something to everyone. And before asking a colony to work that out, a nursery that teaches the carrying on its own: one baby who can't get hungry, three droppings lying in its nest, and a point for each one carried out past a midden line a metre beyond the nest's edge, drawn in the viewer as a dashed ring (`configs/nursery-tidy.toml`). Each one taken out goes, and another turns up in the nest. The midden line moves out in steps: 2.5 m (`nursery-tidy-2.toml`), then 4 m (`-4`), each step trained from the brain that learned the one before.

Step 1, 4M samples on the Mac each, scored on 64 episodes of 90 s, sampled:

| brain | picked up each | taken out each | metres moved away |
|---|---|---|---|
| random | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| the 0.3 brain, untrained | 10.6 | 1.2 | 3.6 |
| fresh, 3e-4 | 87.0 | 43.2 | 76.2 |
| the 0.3 brain, 5e-5 | 71.9 | 9.6 | 17.9 |

- The fresh brain learned it in under 1M samples (reward 0 until about 0.6M, then flat at 0.023 a sample from 1M on) and the 0.3 brain hardly did: a brain that has learned to bring things home isn't the easy start for taking things out.
- What it learned is shuttle runs: take the nearest dropping just past the line, and turn back for the one the nursery puts in the nest the moment it scores. It never takes one further than it has to. It goes out in hops: each pick-up lasts about 0.25 s, then it sets the dropping down about 0.4 m on and picks it up again (87 pick-ups a game for 43 points). Carry is a switch, and setting down puts the dropping just in front, so flicking the switch still moves it forward. The holds are as short with yes/no actions taken by the mean, so it's the brain, not the noise of drawing them. At 4× a hold is a frame or two, so the hops are only visible slowed down.
- So this nursery pays for crossing a line, not for a clean nest, and the instant refill makes the shortest trip the best one. That's a likely reason it didn't help the colonies (below). A nursery closer to what colonies need would pay for the nest being clear, with droppings arriving at a colony's rate and nothing refilled.
- The 0.3 brain first went at 3e-4, as fresh brains do, and one update took it to a KL of 0.2–0.9. `kl_stop` can't help with that, as it only looks after a step. At 5e-5 it started at 0.004–0.017. A trained brain wants the lower rate even in a small nursery, as the rival brains did.

Each later step is 4M more from the brain before at 5e-5, all scored at 4 m (taken out each, 64 episodes): step 1's brain 8.9, step 2's (2.5 m) 15.6, step 3's (4 m) 16.4. At 2.5 m, step 2's takes out 23.2 against step 1's 16.2. The ladder pays, and most of it comes from the middle step: 4M at 4 m added only a tenth.

### Holding on

The hops were cheap, not clever: setting a dropping down and picking it straight up again cost nothing, while carrying it slowed the baby by a tenth and cost energy, so a flickering carry switch did as well as holding on (124 pick-ups a game at 4 m, each held about a sixth of a second, and nothing pushed along the ground). So picking up or putting down now stands a Terrikin still for a moment (`[carry] stoop`, 0.25 s in `configs/nursery-tidy-hold.toml`), and a pick-up costs a little energy (`lift`, 0.01), so hopping is the slow, dear way. Step 3's brain carried on there, 8M at 1e-4, two seeds on the Mac. Scored on 64 held-out episodes, each:

| brain | world | picked up | taken out |
|---|---|---|---|
| step 3's | the 4 m line, no stoop | 123 | 16.6 |
| step 3's | with stoop | 130 | 6.2 |
| hold, seed 1 | with stoop | 16.7 | 15.7 |
| hold, seed 2 | with stoop | 17.7 | 16.0 |

The stoop cost the hopping brain nearly two thirds of its score, and 8M later both seeds pick each dropping up about once and carry it all the way past the line, taking out as many as the hopper did when hops were free. Holding on was always there to learn; nothing paid for it.

### More arenas a rollout

A pod steps 256 tidy arenas at once nearly three times as fast as 64 (samples a second), so the question was whether a sample from a wide rollout teaches as much. The tidy nursery's first step (the 1 m line, from nothing, 4.2M, two packed on a pod), scored on 64 held-out episodes, taken out a game:

| arenas a rollout | samples | taken out |
|---|---|---|
| 64 | 4.2M | 43.0 / 45.0, and 46–49 on the next night's pods |
| 256 | 4.2M | 24.0 / 27.6 |
| 256 | 12M (the same wall clock) | 49.4 |
| 256, epochs 4 | 4.2M | 0.36 (fell apart) |
| 256, lr 6e-4 | 4.2M | 40.7 |
| 128, minibatches 16 | 4.2M | 45.5 |

At 256 arenas there are a quarter as many updates for the same samples, and it needs about three times the samples to catch up: the speed is a wash. Four passes over each wide batch broke it, and a bigger step got most of the way back but not all. 128 arenas nearly matches 64 a sample, on one seed: worth a second before it's a default. Since the KL per update hardly changes with the batch, learning looked capped by updates, not samples, so the other way round was tried next: the same 64 arenas and 4.2M, with rollouts of 64 and 32 steps instead of 128 (two and four times the updates), two seeds each on the Mac:

| steps a rollout | updates | taken out |
|---|---|---|
| 128 | 512 | 49.3 / 45.5 |
| 64 | 1,024 | 40.7 / 44.6 |
| 32 | 2,048 | 0.0 (stopped picking up) / 45.9 |

More updates bought nothing, and at 32 steps one seed fell apart. 64 arenas × 128 steps is where this world learns best a sample: four times fewer updates (256 arenas) costs three times the samples, and more updates than that doesn't help. On the Mac shorter rollouts are also slower (9.9k → 7.7k → 6.9k samples a second) since the update is a bigger share of each round.

### A midden spot

Past a line all the way round the nest, anywhere out of the nest counts as tidy. Ants keep one midden, so the next levels make it a spot: a patch 1 m across whose middle is 3.5 m past the nest's edge, and a dropping only counts when it's put down on it (`[dung] spot`). What's put there stays on a heap that grows all game, and two lie there from the start so it can be seen (`heap`). In `configs/nursery-tidy-midden.toml` the spot is always due east, behind the block; in `nursery-tidy-wander.toml` it's somewhere new each game, any way round (`wander` 180°), so the first trip is a search. The viewer rings the spot, and the evals time the trips: from the start to a baby's first one taken out, against the time between later ones (a midden found once and remembered would make later trips faster).

From the hold brain, which carries a dropping 7 m but not to the spot (0.09 taken out a game on it), each step on the Mac, 64 held-out episodes each:

| hold, seed 2 | | 0.09 | 0.02 | |

| level 1, seed 2 | 8M, fixed spot | 0.00 | | |
| level 1 again, seed 1 | 16M more from level 1 s1, fixed | 8.59 | 6.03 | 12.6 s, 9.9 s |

Not memory, though. Wiping level 2's memory every 2 or 5 seconds (`evals --wipe-every`) changes nothing: 10.86 and 10.14 taken out, later trips 7.5 and 7.1 s. The heap is droppings, which are seen on rays, and it grows all game, so it can be found again by sight every trip. Falling trip times can't show memory in this world. On a held-out game where the heap starts behind it, the baby turns, takes the first dropping over inside 4 s and shuttles for the rest of the game, holding each one the whole way. The next level needs the heap out of sight from afar (droppings on it not seen on rays, or only from close by), so that where it is lives only in what the brain remembers; then the wipe test means something.

### Telling the heap from a loose dropping

Watching the wandering-spot brains, they kept walking up to the heap and saying yes to carry beside it, though a dropping on the heap can't be picked up again: on rays the two looked the same. Before hiding the heap, the rays had to tell them apart, so a heaped dropping is now its own kind (schema v18). An older brain loading starts with the new kind as a copy of the old one, so it sees the heap just as before until training changes that. The evals count how often a baby says yes to carry when the only droppings in reach are on the heap (`carry_heap`).

First the whole tidy ladder again from nothing, two seeds, each an unbroken chain on the Mac (the 1 m line through to the wandering spot with memory, about 93M a seed), then 8M more from each with the heap seen. 64 held-out games on the wandering spot:

| brain | taken out a game | picked up | yes to carry at the heap |
|---|---|---|---|
| ladder again, seed 1 | 8.72 | 16.4 | 60% |
| then heap seen, seed 1 | 5.88 | 32.4 | 66% |
| ladder again, seed 2 | 16.98 | 20.1 | 83% |
| then heap seen, seed 2 | 16.88 | 22.2 | 76% |

Seeing the heap didn't help on its own. Seed 1 picks up twice as often and takes fewer out: it puts droppings down short and picks them up again. Seed 2 is where it was, and both still say yes at the heap most of the time. Nothing in the pay says a try at the heap is wasted, beyond the time it takes, so being able to see the difference isn't a reason to act on it. Wiping memory every 2 s changes nothing for any of them. Two seeds and one step, so a hint. The ladder from nothing also shows how far apart two seeds of the same recipe can end: 8.72 and 16.98.

## A brain for each colony: lineages

colony-young with three copies of the 0.3 brain, each learning only from its own colony (`--rivals`, `--fixed-seats`, 5e-5, warmup and `kl_stop` from the rival fix), 48M samples (16M a brain), three seeds; scored alone in colony-young and head to head (every brain in every seat over the same worlds), held out, 32 episodes.

## Time with nothing to do

What do the colony brains do when they're fed and have time to spare? Four ways of feeding the young colonies: as they are, a feast (66 berries back in 2–4 s), a glut (120, back in 1–2 s), and mealtimes (44, each back only 80–90 s after it's eaten, so food comes in waves). Measured over an episode of 16 arenas (`boredom/probe.py` in the lab notes): the share of time a Terrikin is fed (energy over 0.8 and a full crop), and what it does then.

| brain | fed (young, feast, glut, meals) | fed: spinning on the spot | fed: sprinting | fed: carrying a berry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the 0.3 brain | 3%, 17%, 19%, 6% | 13% young, 41% feast, 51% glut | 0 | 9% young, 33% feast |
| cy-fresh-mem0-s1 (memory) | 1%, 9%, 11%, 3% | 7–10% | 38–49% | 6% young, 31% feast |

- The 0.3 brain twirls when it's full; the memory brain runs about. Neither does anything with the spare time that it doesn't do already. Once full, nothing in the reward depends on what a Terrikin does, so nothing shapes it then: the spin is what its outputs settle on with nothing pulling them anywhere.
- Not tried yet: cleaning in the spare time, droppings and a feast together. As set up it likely wouldn't pay: dropping sickness costs energy, and in a feast food puts it straight back. Mess has to cost something food can't fix (slower hatching in a dirty nest, sickness that slows or shortens a life, or a clean nest in the colony's reward) before spare time has a reason to go to cleaning.
- 24M in the feast at 5e-5 took the twirling from 41% to 14% of its fed time, and it spends more of it at home.
- With fun on as well (novelty 1 for moving blocks, variety 0.02 for trying actions), nothing changed: the young colonies' two blocks can't be moved, so there was nothing to play with.
- So a feast with toys (`colony-toys.toml`: four light boxes to push or carry), 24M each from the 0.3 brain. The brain that found the hole let its colonies starve: all 48 held-out colonies died, after about 94 s on average (every other brain's lived the full 6 minutes), and it ate from the middle 55 times against about 310.

### Holes they found

- **A box that teleports** (fun on in the feast with toys). A box carried off the ground waits at (10000, 10000) until it's set down, and novelty paid for each metre a touched block moved, so every set-down "moved" a box 14 km right beside whoever set it down. ## Capture the flag again: pickups that pay, and past selves to play

The 300M self-play run above learned never to touch a flag. ## The tree nursery: off the flat

Everything so far has been flat: bodies push each other about on a 2D floor. A friend watching the tidy babies asked for a little tree whose fruit they'd have to jump for, which is a good first step up. The physics stays 2D, but each Terrikin now has a height too (`[jump]`, `terrarium/jump.py`): a yes to the new `jump` action while standing launches it fast enough to rise `height` (0.8 m), gravity brings it down, and it steers as it goes. Nothing bumps into anything up there; height only decides what it can reach. The brain feels its own height (`height`, schema v17).

The tree is a game (`[tree]`, `terrarium/tree.py`): one block is a trunk, every apple grows hanging `near`–`far` m from it (0.8–1.1), `reach` m above what a baby standing under it can get to, and an apple goes to whoever is that high under it, hungry or not, for `points` (1). `nursery-tree`: one baby who can't starve, 8 m across, the tree in the middle and the nest off to one side, three apples at a time growing back in 3–6 s, 90 s games. The viewer draws the trunk as an apple tree with a branch out to each apple, and lifts a jumping baby off the ground.

## Two jobs in one world, a night that went wrong

The night of 2 November tried one bigger nursery where the babies had to do two things at once: tidy droppings onto the midden spot and bring apples home from the tree, both far enough off to need remembering. It grew the world and merged two jobs in the same step, and the night went on patching symptoms. The code (`configs/nursery-merged.toml`, a `merged` game, `[reward] wane`) stayed on a branch that never merged; this is what it showed.

12 m arena, held-out 32 games, apples home a minute / droppings taken out a minute:

| brain | apples home | droppings out |
|---|---|---|
| the tidy brain (heap sight), untrained here | 0.0 | 10.7 |
| the tree brain (nest 2.1 m away), untrained here | 7.1 | 0.0 |
| 8M here from the tidy brain | 0.0 | 9.8 |
| 8M here from the tree brain | 12.8 | 0.0 |
| the tidy brain after the tree nursery's steps, in this world | 0.0 | 12.6 |
| the tree brain after the tidy line steps (1 m, 2.5 m) | 15.6 | 0.0 |
| best after a third bridge (tree at the nest's edge for a step) | 0.5 | 7.5 |

- 8M in the two-job world never finds the other job: a tidy brain doesn't jump for apples, a tree brain doesn't pick up droppings.
- Taught the other job first, a step at a time, the tree brain learned the 1 m line (reward a sample 3.4 → 13.4 ×10⁻³), then dropped tidying once apples paid more. It isn't only exploration: points add up and time is shared, so doing the better-paying job alone is the best a brain can do. Nothing paid for doing both.
- `[reward] wane` was the quick fix: each job's points × 1 / (1 + done this game / wane), so a mix beats one job. It didn't bring back a skill already lost (a brain that had stopped tidying stayed stopped), and tidying learned at a line round the nest didn't carry over to the heap spot.
- The tidy brain had never unloaded anything in the nest, so the missing piece for apples was carrying one home. A step with the tree right at the nest's edge got the first brain doing a bit of both (0.5 apples home a minute), and 8M more barely moved it.
- On up a ladder to 24 m, apples were gone from 16 m on. At 24 m one seed's later dung trips were quicker kept than wiped (57 s then 21 s, against 39 s then 35 s): a hint of the heap remembered, one seed.

What it taught us about how we run these, written down the next morning:
- **One change a rung.** The night grew the world and merged two jobs at once, then fixed each thing that broke with a new dial. Every rung since changes one thing from a world the brain already does well in, and skills are only combined once each works alone at the size the combined world asks.
- **The horizon was about 3.5 s.** A choice every 0.05 s with gamma 0.99 halves a reward's worth every ~69 choices, and memory is trained within 128-choice rollouts (6.4 s). Trips to an out-of-sight tree or heap take 10–60 s. (The next section tests it.)
- **Points that add up make specialists.** The fix that fits the world is needs: pay for what eating does to a hungry body, so both jobs matter through staying alive, not through points. It became the needs nursery.
- **Fine-tuning one skill after another forgets the first.**
- **No compass.** The home sense says how far and which way off the heading, but nothing says which way the world is, so "the heap was over there" had no cheap form. Schema v19 added one.

## A choice every 0.2 s

The tree rung where the nest sits 2.8 m from the trunk (world c) had beaten every brain at a choice every 0.05 s (`repeat = 1`): both seeds stopped jumping. Choosing every 0.2 s (`repeat = 4`) stretches the reward's half-life to ~14 s and the memory window to ~26 s, and costs a quarter of the brain passes a world second. From the 1.4 m rung's best brain (0.2 apples home a minute at 2.8 m), held-out 32 games, apples home a minute:

| | world time | seeds | apples home a minute |
|---|---|---|---|
| picker | | | 15.6 |
| a choice every 0.05 s | 8M ticks | 2 | 0.0, 0.0 |
| a choice every 0.05 s | 16M ticks | 2 | 0.0, 0.0 |
| a choice every 0.2 s | 16M ticks (4M choices) | 4 | 10.3, 8.0, 3.2, 9.9 |
| a choice every 0.2 s, gamma 0.995 | 16M ticks | 2 | 12.6, 3.6 |

- With the same world time, every seed choosing every 0.05 s gives up on the 2.8 m carry (4 of 4), and every seed choosing every 0.2 s learns it (6 of 6, 3.2 to 12.6, spread wide). Gamma 0.995 on top made no clear difference.
- It isn't free everywhere. ## A tree they go back to: what the notes hold

Where the notes above stop, the far tree (32 m arena, sight 4 m, the trunk anywhere up to ±6 m from its usual spot, 90 s games) had been learned without memory: the brain's own search matched or beat a script that remembers where it last saw the trunk (`tree_seen`), notes kept or wiped. Scripted headroom, measured on 5 November, said why. Finding the tree is the cost, remembering it pays only after, and wherever finding is hard enough for memory to matter the scripts never reach home in about half the games (34 of 64 at ±12 m). Longer games added little (`tree_seen` less `searcher`: +0.46 apples home a minute at 90 s, +0.49 at 180, +0.58 at 270). A longer reach for credit (GAE `lam` 0.99; 0.95 has a half-life of 2.3 s) made a better searcher, not a rememberer: at ±6 m it brought two seeds to 4.82 and 4.84 (parent 4.50, picker 4.93), and wiping their notes every 3 s cost 0.01 and 0.17. A review also found that the scorer built each game's world without scattering the trunk, so far-tree scores had been of a tree at its usual spot. Rescored with the fix, every call in the far-tree ladder holds (the picker up 0.1–0.3 a minute, the best seed 0.0–0.2), and so do the tag tables; the far tree at ±6 m drops below the picker (4.25 kept, 4.23 wiped, picker 4.83, 32 games), and the spread table above wasn't rescored.

**What the review found in the scorer, and what changed** (#115). Five things:

- One random stream served every arena, the apple regrowth and the burrow's heading, so a brain's moves changed later draws in other games. Every game now has its own streams from its seed and number, so game i of a seed is the same game in a job of any size.
- A brain's sampled action noise began at a fixed seed whatever the world's, so a fresh layout wasn't fresh noise. It is per game now.
- The wipe on unloading fired on any drop in the crop at the nest, eating included, and could miss an unload followed by a refill. It fires on the store going up.
- `start` fell back to the nest spawn after 50 failed tries without saying, and `walked` counted the burrow's jump. Tree games now report per game the fallbacks, the failed moves and whether the tree is in sight from home, and `walked` leaves the jump out.
- Seed ranges used for choosing brains are retired for confirming (held-out 2,000,000, 7,100,000 to 7,600,000, 9,000,000 and 9,000,001); a confirm takes a block never used before (8,101,000 on).

## Two passes or four

Each update goes over the rollout's samples four times (`epochs = 4`). Halving it to two would make the update ~40% quicker, if the brains learn as well. A small grid on the Mac, 31 October: peekaboo with a second look (`nursery-peek-again`), fresh brains without memory, 4M samples, three seeds each of `epochs` 4, `epochs` 2, and `epochs` 4 with `kl_stop` 0.02 (an update stops early once the policy has moved that far). Held-out 64 games, berries found a minute (random 0.63, a remember-where-you-last-saw-it picker 13.08):

| | seed 1 | seed 2 | seed 3 | update time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| epochs 4 | 1.44 | 0.80 | 0.17 | 26–30 min |
| epochs 2 | 1.48 | **1.45** | **0.42** | 15–18 min |
| epochs 4, kl_stop 0.02 | 1.64 | 0.75 | 0.17 | as epochs 4 |

- Two passes did as well or better in every seed (seeds 2 and 3 outside the intervals) for about 60% of the update time.
- `kl_stop` 0.02 stopped an update early in only 1–2 of 512: from fresh at 3e-4 the KL sits at 0.005–0.02. It's for forks and rivals, where the first updates jump (above), not fresh starts.
- Only a hint. These are brains without memory in a memory game after 4M, with tiny scores, and seed 3 is below random in every arm: the spread between seeds is bigger than the difference between arms. Worth repeating in a world the brains do well in before changing the default.

## Still to try

- **The tree, bigger and with memory:** a 16 m nursery with three trees whose apples grow back slowly, so remembering which tree still has fruit pays, scored with memory wiped every few seconds and kept; then snacks (a hungry baby slows down until it eats an apple, so eating one now competes with carrying it home).
- **The memory line's next steps, one change each, free machines first** (the second review's plan, with a kill rule at each): take the head start away (rung C above, at most 3 × 8M), then two nestmates (compare each Terrikin's wipe with everyone's at once, and each one's deliveries), then a modest rise in energy drain (it must still eat when it needs to and keep its returns), then the frozen colony exam: the same weights, unchanged, in 256 fresh colony arenas, zero training, measuring remembered returns, survival and the store separately, and naming the one behaviour that fails before the next rung is specified.
- **Three memory-only nurseries, where the best that forgets is known:** *last lantern* (two cues one after the other, go to the latest; 50% for a brain that forgets), *depleted patch* (sample one of two patches, come home, then go to the other; remembered absence), and *order garden* (two objects appear in random order, later choose the one that came first; sequence, not navigation). Each needs balanced trials, the same observations and body state after the delay, nothing left in the world to read, and only the first committed choice scored. Peekaboo with a spin stays as the positive control. Before training any of them, build a tiny finite-state version and work out the best memoryless score exactly, then check that the physics version shows the same observations after different cues.
- **A better probe of the heading:** the swap read the set-off direction at 3 m, which is loose even with its own notes. Reading the whole first stretch, or a swap at every deposit and not only the first, would say more.
- **Colony pay, further:** four seeds slowed the drift but none beat the 0.3 brain (above). Left: the same pay for the young colonies' fresh memory brain, which is the best at them, once its second seed is scored.
- **Seat 1's weak brains:** train the lineages with the nests swapped round and see whether the weak brain follows the nest or stays with seat 1; if it stays, look for a colony-index bug in how rewards and episode ends reach each brain.
- **Toys for the fed:** more seeds of the feast with toys and fun (one seed lifted boxes a fifth more), and a toy that does something when played with (a ball that rolls, a box that makes a sound), so novelty has more to find.

- **More ladders.** Second looks further apart taught memory faster than one step did (above): try longer stretches before the last step, a ladder in the maze, and 48M more from a ladder brain.
- **Memory with a side job**, if fresh starts stop being enough (they made peekaboo's notes worth reading, above). A head start for the link back didn't make a forked brain's notes worth reading. The side job from the toy test is next: from the notes, say what was seen a few steps ago, so the notes hold the past before anything pays for it.
- **Runners trained for Catch them!** against a pretend child as it (a scripted chaser that's always it), instead of a tag brain that learned with the it changing hands; and Catch them!'s logs from real games as the yardstick.
- **Hiders that can win** at hide-and-seek: more cover and a longer count, pay for each second unseen, or a second brain for the hiders.
- **A coin bonus per coin**, on eat and sprint only.
- **33 rays** for a game where far-off Terrikins matter and nothing hides them (tag, capture the flag), at twice the training cost.
- **Long runs that don't fall apart.** Anneal the learning rate (or stop an update early past a KL limit) for anything past ~30M, and watch entropy and KL alongside episode length: two memory brains have fallen apart late in flash with the rate held at 2e-4.
- **Do memory brains train better, notes or not?** Ahead in three pairs of five by 0.4–0.8, with their notes cut off as well as with them. Four seeds each of: no memory, 128 notes, and notes that can't learn on whole streams (`memory_lr = 0`), all scored on the same machine.
- **Room to try things.** A full body is about 30 s of walking from starving (1.0 energy, 0.01 a second to live and 0.02 more at full thrust, 0.4 from a berry), and the store hardly helps one Terrikin, since they so rarely eat from it. Trying anything new, like carrying a berry home or pulling at a door, spends that margin, and a brain that tries it while exploring mostly dies before it pays, so PPO learns not to. That fits every dead end so far: the vault doors, carrying with no use, a store nobody eats from. Playing as one shows it too: taking a berry home to see what happens is close to starving. To try: measure the slack each world leaves (the share of time well above hunger, and food eaten against food needed), then give a world more of it in ways a living thing could have (resting costs less, like torpor; a bigger reserve; hunger met from the colony's store, as ants feed each other), and see whether new actions get tried and kept. Worlds that test memory need the pressure that makes remembering pay, so this is a dial per world.

- **Tired, not starving, in the nurseries** (`[tired]`; the first pair at drain 0.016 didn't help, above: next, priced higher). Babies can't starve, so moving costs them nothing and wandering about scores as well as waiting where the berry will show again. With stamina that drains as they move and comes back while they keep still, and a slow crawl below a line, a wander now costs the next find. Each game gets a pair, tired off and on, priced so a flat-out wanderer is tired by about halfway and a baby that walks to a spot and waits never is. Peekaboo's baselines: walking to where it last saw the berry and waiting finds 10.5 a minute; random 0.33; standing still 0.
- **Stamina in the main line**, if the nurseries show it helps: sprints drawing on it instead of a fixed cooldown, and rest at home restoring it, so a forage and a rest are a real choice. Lightly, in a fork of colony.toml: every pressure added in 0.3 shrank the room to try things.
- Spots that move to new places once or twice an episode, so notes have to be rewritten, not just kept.
- Fully random flowers as a control: memory should add little there.
- A flower that wanders: short memory, tracking.
- Food in one or two places that change, with smell off or weak (smell already leads to food without memory).
- Mazes ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md), Mazes track).
- Memory of 32, 64 and 256 notes if 128 shows anything, and longer replays (`steps`) if credit for remembering has to reach further back.
