A job's result can't be lost to its size any more. Every run says where its training time goes. Terrikins can carry a berry (schema v9): a new yes/no action, `carry`, picks up a berry within reach and holds it on top of the head, like an ant with a seed, and a fresh yes puts it down in front, still a berry. It's apart from the crop and the jelly: a carried berry isn't eaten or stored, and nothing else can see it until it's down. Carrying slows a Terrikin a little and costs a little energy, and a new sense says whether it has one. It's on only in the colony world, and nothing rewards it: evals count berries picked up, put down at home, and eaten by a nestmate after being put down, to see whether a brain finds a use for it by itself. Replays mark who's carrying, and the viewer draws the berry on their head.

Brains can have memory. `train --memory N` gives a brain N notes it carries from one choice to the next (a GRU), read back through a link that starts at zero, so it acts exactly as before until training finds a use for them. Notes start blank with each life and are wiped at death. A brain with memory is trained a whole creature's rollout at a time, replayed in order from the notes it began with, and `evals --wiped` scores it with its notes blanked before every choice, to show whether it uses them.

`configs/spots.toml` is the first world built to need memory: one colony, no nights, little food, and nectar flowers that open only at two spots drawn anew each episode, 12 m apart (`[flowers] spots`, `spot_gap`; 0 keeps them anywhere). ## 0.3.0: Colony basics

Colonies. Three colonies of six Terrikins forage, carry food home in a crop, store it as jelly, get through three cold nights on it huddled at home, and hatch new Terrikins from what's left. The 0.3 brain (`c1c16fc46934.pt`, from the run `pbt-y1-28`, in `sim/brains.toml`) learned all of it from reward, through forks that copy whichever is doing best. On 64 episodes no training saw, every colony gets through all three nights, and they end the six minutes with 6.40 Terrikins of the 6 they started with (up to 8), where nester, a scripted forager that heads home at dusk, ends with 3.09 and loses 61 of 192 colonies. That's 0.45 more than the brain its line started from, with a quarter fewer deaths. Most of the Terrikins that die still starve with jelly at home, and the 0.3 brain never sprints. Alone in the solo world it eats 6.29 food a minute (the 0.2 brain 5.23 by its mean) but dies in 21 of 64 episodes. The sample replay is `samples/0.3-colonies.jsonl.gz`.

Terrikins now live in a garden under a glass dome on Mars: a clay body with feet and a waddle, a face, a colony-coloured tail, droppings (drawn only), a follow camera (F) and a flyby into the dome (`&intro`). The public site at terrarium.martianriver.com plays the latest brain (`npm run site`). The tools that made the first Instagram reel are in `viewer/reel/`. Droppings are smaller now. `viewer/reel/0.3.json` is the second reel's script, and `viewer/reel/sound.py` gives a reel sound that matches what's on screen (bites, unloads, deaths, day and night ambience, music).

Training got faster and accounted for. torch has CPU and CUDA extras on Linux, `--device` and `--workers` split episodes over processes, and `terrarium.remote` trains on a rented GPU from a committed checkout. `terrarium.compute` adds up the hours of every run and the dollars of every rented machine (`sim/compute.toml`), and a promoted brain records what it took. The runs page (`runs.html`, with `terrarium.serve`) lists every run with its curves, live while it trains, and each checkpoint as a generation to watch.

`train --rivals` trains several brains side by side in one world, one to a colony, each taking every seat over the arenas and learning only from its own colonies.

The brain sees more (schema v2): the distance and direction to its own nest, whether a creature it sees is a nestmate, and how far its own and other nests are along each ray. The 0.2 brain loads and acts the same.

Colonies carry food home (schema v3). Food eaten past full goes into a crop, like an ant's social stomach, and slows the carrier down. Two new actions: unload, which puts the crop into the nest's store, or onto the ground as jelly anyone can eat, and eat, from the crop or (when hungry, at home) from the store. Two new inputs say how full the crop is and whether it's home. Food grows in patches that move each episode. The reward pays for storing, can be partly shared with the colony, and pays a little for carrying food closer to home (and takes it back for walking off), which only moves reward earlier. `carrier` is the new scripted baseline: greedy that takes a full crop home. A new yes/no action now starts out mostly no, so it's tried now and then; zero-padding it made it fire half the time in training, which is why nothing stored for the first eight runs (`docs/notes/0.3.md` has the whole story).

A dead Terrikin's body now leaves the world: nothing bumps into it or sees it. The viewer puts a small tombstone where it fell, which fades after 20 s. The replay clock runs on into hours and days.

The viewer can follow training: `/?latest` plays the newest checkpoint of each live run in turn (or `/?latest=text`, of the newest run with that in its name) and looks for a newer one each time the replay ends.

Evals v2: errors are taken over arenas rather than creatures, each number has a p10, and colonies get jelly eaten, deaths with jelly in their own store, colonies lost, and how often `unload` and `eat` say yes where they matter. A brain's `unload` and `eat` are drawn at the odds it gives them, in evals and replays, while it steers by its mean; `--modes mean` and `--modes sampled` score it all by the mean or all drawn as in training, and training ends by scoring it all three ways. `--held-out` plays the worlds kept for promotion.

Running out of time in training is no longer counted as an end: with `[ppo] bootstrap`, a creature cut off alive at the time limit is paid what its value says the rest is worth. Train configs can now hold true/false settings.

Brain schema v4: a Terrikin senses how much jelly its colony has, and how light it is. Older brains load with no weight on either and act as before. A train config's `[start] fresh` starts named actions of the parent brain over, as new actions start.

Nights, off unless a world turns them on (`[night]`): at nightfall the food standing goes and none grows back until dawn, the light fades at dusk and comes back after dawn, and the dark is cold, draining energy faster except in the nest or close to nestmates. Replays of worlds with nights carry the light. Evals count the nights each creature lives through, deaths in the dark and colonies still going at the first dawn, and `nester` joins the scripted baselines: carrier that heads home at dusk and waits out the night in the nest. A train config's `[start] staggered` starts each training arena at a random time of day, so a batch isn't all day or all night; evals still start at dawn.

A choice can be held for several ticks (`[ppo] repeat`): rewards over those ticks are summed, `gamma` and `lam` then count choices rather than ticks, and a brain trained that way acts that way in evals and replays. `[ppo] anneal = false` keeps the learning rate constant, for training in chunks. Two new rewards pay for warmth (`snug` at any time, `warm` in the dark), `train --no-evals` skips the end evals, and `train --mix WORLD:ARENAS` trains in arenas of other worlds beside the main one (solo ones, so a colony brain keeps foraging alone). Evals cap torch's threads on rented machines like training does.

The runs page shows the compute behind each brain: hours and dollars per machine for its run and every run behind its parent, back to the first, plus all training so far per machine, rented machines that never ran anything included. A rented machine's bill is split across the runs it hosted. Run records now keep the CPU, memory and the parent checkpoint's own notes (which run and how far in).

Creatures can grab a block now: a yes/no action (schema v6, `[grab]`) that takes hold of the nearest block within reach, or the first to come that close, for 2 s from the last yes, and drags it along, for a little energy a second. The brain senses how much of its grip is left. It's off in every world but a new experiment, `configs/vault.toml`: the night world with half the food (`[food] middle`) growing in a room walled in by blocks too heavy to move, behind two doors that can only be pulled out. Evals count food eaten from the middle, blocks moved, how often grab says yes with a block in reach, deaths away from the nest in daylight (foragers that couldn't feed themselves), and the share of meals eaten with another colony close by; `eat` at home now counts a hungry carrier that unloads and eats on the same tick, and `--policy` on its own scores only the brains given. Food keeps clear of a block's own box instead of a circle round it, so food spots drawn for a seed changed in every world with blocks. The viewer has a `&lite` mode for laptops: one pixel per point, no shadows or bloom, 30 frames a second.

Moving costs more than thrust now. Turning costs energy by its square (`[energy] turn`), so steering is close to free and spinning on the spot isn't, and sprint is a new yes/no action (schema v5, `[sprint]`): a burst at 1.6 times top speed for 1.5 s, costing energy by how hard it runs, then 6 s before the next. The brain senses how much of a burst is left and how rested it is. Both are on in `configs/night.toml` (which also now gives jelly the 0.4 the night brains trained on, and grows half its food in the middle, between the nests, `[food] middle`) and off in the other worlds. A train config's `[reward] effort` charges the energy spent moving as it's spent, in food's worth. Evals count how often sprint says yes when it can, and how hard brains turn out by day and in the nest at night. Old run records still rebuild, with turning free and sprint off.

Training runs can go through a job queue (`docs/compute.md`): I sign each job, a coordinator hands jobs out, and workers on my machines, rented GPUs and later lent ones check the signature and every input, train, and send the run back. Donated machines only run code that's public on one of the repo's branches, never from a pull request. Rented machines have their own kind of token, and a batch can say `on = "rented"` to keep its jobs off my own machines (or `"trusted"` to keep them on).

Workers can run on a friend's machine, Windows included, and keep out of its owner's way: they train only after 10 minutes with no keyboard or mouse and on mains power, pause the job the moment someone's back or the GPU passes 85 °C, give it back to the queue when a game or anything full screen starts, and use half the CPU at low priority (a hard cap on Windows). Each rule is a flag or a line in the worker's `settings.toml`; rented machines skip them. The worker's status says what it's doing and why, and so do its beats to the coordinator. Time a job sat paused is left out of its hours. A machine keeps the runs it made. A batch can split a job into chained pieces (`chunk = N`), so a lent machine only holds short jobs. The coordinator limits each token to 60 requests a minute and runs on a bare Python, without numpy or torch. A worker stopped by two signals at once (`uv run` passes its own on) still hands its jobs back.

Every minute a worker reports what its machine is (OS, CPU, cores, memory, NVIDIA GPUs) and what it's doing, working or not; the coordinator keeps only known fields, through a restart too, and `terrarium.jobs workers` lists them. `/api/board` is public: per machine its name, kind (mine, rented or lent), GPU, samples, hours, jobs done and the day it was last active, never whether its owner is at it.

The coordinator has an admin page, `/api/admin`, signed in with Google for listed addresses only (code flow with PKCE, nothing about the account kept): make a token for a handle, give it a new one, revoke it, and see every machine with what it is (GPU and its memory, CPU, OS, memory), its worker version and how its jobs ended (done, lost, failed, given back). A worker checks a known signature when it starts and stops with the reason if it can't; on Windows it uses the `ssh-keygen` that comes with Git, since Windows 10's own may predate signature checks (worker version 5). The site has a page for lending a GPU, `/at-home/`: what the worker does on someone's machine, how to install it, its versions, reviews of its code by models with no stake in it (and the prompt to run your own), and the board, read live from the queue, with what lent machines have trained priced at what rented GPUs cost. `/api/board` may be cached for 30 seconds. While a job trains, a worker keeps its machine from sleeping (`keep_awake`, on by default; the screen can still turn off), since it trains just when nobody's at the machine; and a job taken away from it (its lease ran out, say over a sleep) now ends with every process it started, where before its rollout and eval processes held the GPU and the worker waited on them as another program. The worker's docs now say uv keeps its own download cache outside the worker's folder. The Brains page's steps reach hatching, and what's being worked on is colonies with room to grow.

terrarium.martianriver.com has a front page: what the Terrikins are, where they're at, what's next, and a blog: write-ups of what I tried in plain words with the details folded away. The viewer moved to `/watch/`; old links with a replay in them still get there. Posts are Markdown in `site/`, built by `npm run site`; drafts never go in git, and `npm run review` builds a copy with them in, looking just as they will when published, for reading on a phone before anything goes up. Each post names the model that drafted it and says I edited it, and /how/ says who does what. Short clips are cut from my backups at build time, so videos stay out of the repo too (`docs/site.md`). A Brains page lists every brain the latest came from, back to the first, with the newest from the training going on now; each one plays in the world it trained in, and you can step from one to the next in the viewer (`terrarium.gallery`). The Brains page starts with what's being worked on now, the vault first: its best tries so far, each with what it tried. On the site, the viewer's Open replay button is a Brains link instead, since a file picker means nothing on a phone.

The repo has a README, the MIT licence, and issue templates for bugs and ideas (issues are welcome; pull requests aren't taken).

Notes on what was tried in each stage, dead ends included, are in `docs/notes/`.

The compute ledger names my own machines (the MacBook Air, 15-inch, M3, 16 GB) and each rented pod's GPU and CPU, and counts a pod's whole rental, setup and idle time included, next to its training hours, on the runs page too.

Food has a smell (schema v7, `[smell]`, on in the vault only): it spreads over a grid on each arena, fades, and stops at blocks, except a door, which lets some through. A Terrikin senses how strong it is and which way it gets stronger, so a shut room's food can be found from outside. Old brains load and act the same.

`train --fast` compiles the PPO update and runs the brain's forward pass in bfloat16 on CUDA. `terrarium.widen` makes a wider copy of a brain that starts out acting the same (each unit copied, a little noise to part the copies), so a bigger brain can carry on from a smaller one's training.

`train --gpu-collect` keeps the brain off the rollout workers: they only step their worlds, and the brain picks every worker's actions at once on the GPU.

Evals score each policy, and each brain in each mode, side by side in its own process, and can run the brains on a GPU (`--device`); training's own evals at the end do both, which took twice as long as the training in the vault.

Colonies hatch (`[hatch]`): one that has lost someone, with enough jelly in its store, pays some of it for an egg in the nest, and a full-grown Terrikin hatches in the lost one's place. An egg needs a living nestmate to tend it. `configs/colony.toml` is 0.3's main world from here: the night world with hatching and food smell on. Training can pay a colony for each one it hatches (`[reward] hatch`). Evals count hatchlings, colonies that hatched any, and colonies still going at the end of a full episode; deaths are counted per death, and a colony is lost if it was ever left with nobody. Hatchlings get new ids in replays.

Colonies can grow: `[colonies] room` gives each colony places past the ones it starts with, empty until it hatches into them, so a colony with a full store grows instead of only replacing the dead. `configs/colony.toml` has room for two more, up to eight. Eggs are laid where their Terrikin will hatch, and replays carry them (`eggs`): the viewer draws each one speckled in its colony's colour on the nest, rocking as it gets close, then cracking open as the hatchling, small at first, grows to full size. Evals count colony size at the end and at its peak, and keep the other numbers per starting place. `terrarium.versus` puts a different policy or brain in each colony of the same arenas, every one in every seat in turn, to see which does better against the others. A brain's yes/no actions can be coins with odds it sets (`terrarium.yesno` turns a brain over, keeping about the odds it had), instead of a Gaussian cut at 0.

The vault's door experiments are gone again: hinged blocks, `configs/vault-swing.toml`, `[reward] door` and `[start] ajar`/`opening`. Old run records with hinged doors load with doors that slide.

`configs/blocks.toml` is a probe world: the night world with a ring of 16 light blocks round the middle food, which a Terrikin can push by walking into them.

Training can pay a little fun (`[reward] novelty`, `variety`, off by default): for each metre a block moves while a Terrikin touches or holds it, and for saying yes to an action it hasn't said yes to lately. Both wear off the more it's done, like boredom, and come back after a while.

Colonies have food enough to grow: `colony.toml` has 22 places food grows (14 was too lean: Terrikins starved turning in circles far from home), walking is slower (2.0 m/s) and the sprint twice that. Nectar flowers (`[flowers]`, on in `colony.toml`): now and then by day a flower opens somewhere clear of the nests and closes again after 15 s or at nightfall; the first to touch one drinks three foods' worth, eaten or carried, and runs at 1.25× for 8 s. Brain schema v8: flowers are their own kind on the rays and a Terrikin feels its boost. Replays carry the open flowers and each Terrikin's boost and sprint, and the viewer draws them: a lorikeet-coloured flower with a glow, drawn at its size in the sim and hoovered into the mouth of whoever eats it (one that closes by itself wilts), a happy face with a shimmering antenna and jelly belly on nectar, and dust behind a sprint (`&preview` lays make-believe ones over any replay). A run now stops if git can't say what code it is, where it used to record an empty commit as clean. `&ant` in the viewer shows a sketch of an ant built to learn to walk: rigid parts, a motor at each leg joint, feet that sense the ground, and its centre of mass over the feet (J hides the markers); it walks a hand-made gait, as nothing in the sim has legs yet. ## 0.2.0: Solo forager

The first trained brain. One creature alone in the arena with 8 berries learns to keep itself fed. On the evals it eats 18.4 berries a minute and lives out every 3-minute episode. Random actions manage 0.26 and starve in under a minute. A scripted forager that beelines for the nearest berry it sees gets 13.8, so the brain beats that too.

The sim gained what a brain needs. Each creature casts 16 rays over 200°, out to 8 m, and gets back a distance and what was hit: wall, creature, food or block. Running out of energy now kills, though the body stays where it fell. Episodes end when everyone is dead or 3 minutes are up, and each arena restarts on its own. `configs/solo.toml` is the one-creature world. Rays are the expensive part: at 64 arenas with three colonies they cost 14× the physics (`docs/benchmark.md`). Solo, they cost about the same as the physics.

The brain is a small transformer, about 70k parameters, that attends over its own state plus one token per ray, with a type for each thing it can see. Later stages add types and inputs instead of reshaping it. What it sees and outputs is an append-only schema (`sim/terrarium/schema.py`). A saved brain has to match the start of today's lists, and loading one that doesn't fails with the place they part. Newer inputs load zeroed, so an older brain keeps working (`docs/brain.md`).

Training is PPO, with episodes played on the CPU and learning on the Mac's GPU. Every run gets a folder under `runs/` (outside git) with its commit, both configs, parent, schema, machine, versions, per-update metrics, checkpoints and final evals. Uncommitted code only trains as a scratch run. `terrarium.promote` takes a finished run from committed code on `next` that clearly beats random, backs the weights up outside the repo, and adds them to `sim/brains.toml`, the lineage. The 0.2 brain is `17cc35ad7932` there, and its weights are attached to this release (`docs/training.md`).

Evals v1 (`docs/evals.md`) score any policy on 64 fixed-seed episodes no run trains on: food per minute and seconds alive. "Clearly beats" means twice random's food and both numbers ahead by over three standard errors. `terrarium.record --brain` writes a replay of a brain. `samples/0.2-forager.jsonl.gz` is this version's sample, a full episode of the promoted brain.

torch is a new dependency: the CPU build on Linux, so CI doesn't download CUDA.

## 0.1.0: Sim core and replays

The real sim, headless in Python. Three colonies of six creatures on a 26 m arena with walls, nests, 28 berries that regrow somewhere new after they're eaten, and two blocks heavy enough that pushing one takes effort. Creatures steer with thrust and turn, collide and shove through pymunk, and burn energy as they go, though running out does nothing yet. For now their actions are random. Every balance number is in `sim/configs/world.toml`, and the loader refuses anything missing, unknown or the wrong type.

Physics was picked by measuring. A vectorised numpy sim and pymunk ran the same scene. pymunk was six times faster at 64 arenas and brings the static friction and joints later stages need. The cost is a few cm of overlap between bodies, which doesn't show in the viewer (`docs/physics.md`). The speed baseline is 1,432 ticks a second for 64 arenas on the M3 (`docs/benchmark.md`).

Runs are saved as replays: gzipped JSON lines, a header then one frame per tick, format v1 (`docs/replays.md`). The viewer opens them with a button or by dropping the file in, and loops them. The scripted demo is still what the viewer opens on. `samples/0.1-random.jsonl.gz` is this version's sample, and every later viewer has to play it.

CI runs the sim's tests and the viewer's tests, and has the sim write a replay that the viewer's loader then reads.

## 0.0.0: Visual demo

A 3D viewer for the world, with a scripted stand-in for the sim. Three colonies of six blobs hop around their nests, eat berries, shove two blocks around and flash signal colours. They squash and stretch, blink, look into their turns, and dim as their energy runs down. The viewer draws whatever sends it frames in the format in `viewer/src/world.ts`, which is what the sim's replays will use from 0.1.
