Before there could be a colony, there had to be one Terrikin that could keep itself alive. That was stage 0.2, codename Clover.

## The rules

One Terrikin, alone in a walled garden with eight berries and a couple of wooden blocks. Moving uses energy, and so does just being alive. A berry tops it up. If its energy runs out, it starves. Each life lasts at most three minutes.

That's all it gets told, and really it isn't even told that much. Nobody explains what a berry is or where to find one. It gets a point for every berry it eats and loses one if it dies, and that's the only feedback there is. Everything else it has to work out by trial and error. This is called reinforcement learning.

It sees through 16 rays fanned out in front of it, like whiskers made of light. Each ray says how far it reached and what it hit: nothing, a wall, a block or a berry. A small neural network turns those 16 answers, plus how much energy it has left and how fast it's going, into two choices: how hard to push forward, and which way to turn.

<details>
<summary>What it sees and how it learns, in more detail</summary>

- The rays cover 200° and reach 8 m, in an arena 26 m across.
- The brain is a small transformer, 68,229 weights. It looks at one token for itself (energy, speed, what it did last tick) and one per ray, and decides where to pay attention. Later stages add new kinds of things to see without rebuilding it.
- Training is PPO. The reward is +1 for each berry and −1 for dying. There's deliberately no reward for staying alive: paying for time alone teaches it to stand still.
- More in the repo: [docs/brain.md](https://github.com/MartianRiver/Terrarium/blob/HEAD/docs/brain.md) and [docs/training.md](https://github.com/MartianRiver/Terrarium/blob/HEAD/docs/training.md).

</details>

## Practice

It practised in 256 gardens at once on my MacBook Air. 52 minutes of training added up to 11.6 days of practice in the Terrikin's own time, over 6,493 lives. I saved its brain along the way, and each save is a generation.

**Generation 0** hasn't practised at all. It moves at random and starves in about a minute.

**Generation 1**, 3.5 minutes of training in (23 hours of practice), has found the berries, then wanders off and loses track of them.

**Generation 2**, 7 minutes in (46 hours of practice), survives the whole three minutes.

**Generation 13** is the final brain. It heads for whatever berry it can see, and moves on to the next.

Most of the learning happened early. By about a sixth of the way through it was eating nearly as much as it ever would, and the rest of the run was polish.

## How good is it?

I tested it on 64 lives in gardens it had never trained in, against two others. One just moves at random. The other is a little script I wrote that heads straight for the nearest berry it can see, which shows what the 16 rays make possible. The trained brain eats 18.4 berries a minute and never starved. The script manages 13.8, and random moves get 0.26.

So it beats the script I wrote for it. Even its worst life of the 64 beats the script's average.

<details>
<summary>The numbers</summary>

64 lives of up to 180 s each, on seeds no training run uses:

| | berries a minute | seconds alive |
|---|---|---|
| random moves | 0.26 ± 0.07 | 49.6 ± 2.1 |
| the script (heads for the nearest berry) | 13.79 ± 0.16 | 180.0 ± 0.0 |
| the 0.2 brain | 18.40 ± 0.14 | 180.0 ± 0.0 |

Its worst life was 14.7 berries a minute. The brain's id is `17cc35ad7932`, and its weights are attached to the [v0.2.0 release](https://github.com/MartianRiver/Terrarium/releases/tag/v0.2.0). How the tests work: [docs/evals.md](https://github.com/MartianRiver/Terrarium/blob/HEAD/docs/evals.md).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why eight berries</summary>

I picked the berry count by running random moves and the script at a few counts. At 8, random moves starve within a minute, and the script lives the whole three minutes. So time alive tells good foraging from bad as clearly as the berry count does. With fewer, even good foraging starves sometimes; with more, there's little to tell apart.

| berries | random | the script |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | 0.86 a minute, 70 s alive | 37.2, 180 s |
| 12 | 0.46, 57 s | 19.6, 180 s |
| 8 | 0.26, 50 s | 13.8, 180 s |
| 5 | 0.15, 47 s | 9.0, 174 s (some die) |

</details>

<details>
<summary>What it cost, and how big a brain is that?</summary>

Every run that went into it, the ones I threw away included, was 1.1 hours of training on my MacBook Air (15-inch, M3, 16 GB unified memory). Nothing rented, so $0.

68,229 weights is roughly ten times the synapses of a nematode worm and a seven-hundredth of a fruit fly's. But it does far less than a worm. A worm runs its whole life on its nervous system; this brain only knows how to eat. The comparison and its sources are in [docs/brain.md](https://github.com/MartianRiver/Terrarium/blob/HEAD/docs/brain.md).

</details>
