Stage 0.3 is finished. Three colonies of six Terrikins forage, carry food home, store it as jelly, get through three cold nights huddled on their nests, and hatch new Terrikins from what's left over. Tested on 64 worlds no training ever saw, every colony made it through all three nights, and they ended the six minutes with more Terrikins than they started with.

{{clip colonies-timelapse}}

## Since the nights

At the end of [the last post](/blog/nights/) the colonies headed home at dusk and huddled all night, spinning on the spot, and the plan was less food than they need.

The spinning went once turning cost energy and food got short. Spinning flat out all night costs about half a jelly, and with food for only two colonies' worth, that half a jelly is the difference between the store lasting the night or not. The brain that made it through best mostly sits still at home in the dark now, and deaths at night fell to a quarter. Nobody paid it to sit still.

Less food worked too, at first. With food for two colonies, a brain got 87% of colonies through all three nights, where my nester script got about half. But a colony could only hatch into the place of someone who'd died, so the best it could ever do was stay at six. I gave each colony room for two more, trained again, and the numbers went up. Then I watched a replay: five of the eighteen starved in the first minute, turning in circles far from home, with every store empty by the first dusk. The world was too lean for a colony to grow in, and seconds alive could be pushed up by keeping a few alive rather than by growing. So the food went back up, from 14 places it grows to 22, where colonies grow if they forage and eat well and don't if they don't.

I also tried pulling doors open: half the food in a walled room, behind doors that could only be pulled, and a new action to grab hold of a block. They learned to grab on. They never pulled. It turned into a puzzle about doors, and nothing later on has doors, so it's saved for when moving things is the point. What stayed from it is smell: a Terrikin can smell food now, and which way it is, so food behind a block or another Terrikin isn't lost to it.

## Hatching

{{clip colonies-hatch}}

A colony with jelly to spare can buy an egg: with at least 10 in its store, 3 of it goes on an egg in the nest, and 20 seconds later a new Terrikin hatches. It can hatch into the place of one that died, or into one of two spare places, so a colony can grow to eight. An egg needs someone at home to tend it, so a colony with nobody left stays lost. In the sim a hatchling comes out full-grown; the viewer draws it growing, for now.

## Nectar flowers, and a sprint

{{clip colonies-flower}}

Every so often by day, a nectar flower opens somewhere on the lawn. It's worth three foods, it's gone after 15 seconds, and there's only ever one. Walking got slower, and the sprint got stronger to make up for it: twice top speed for a second and a half, then six seconds to rest. A flower is rare, rich and doesn't wait, which is what a sprint is for.

{{clip colonies-turbo}}

Eating one gives eight seconds of turbo mode, a quarter faster, and sparkly.

But mostly they find flowers by luck. The brain I'm releasing eats about as many as nester does, and nester can't even see them. Racing for one means remembering where it was and heading back for it, and these brains can't remember anything. The clips above are from a brain in the other line of training, which still sprints. The 0.3 brain stopped sprinting altogether during training.

<details>
<summary>Why it stopped sprinting</summary>

Sprinting is a yes/no choice. Late in this stage I changed how those are learned: instead of a bell curve cut at zero, each one became a coin with its own odds of yes, so training moves the odds directly. In the line the 0.3 brain comes from, the coin for sprinting slid down to about 1 yes in 500,000 chances. A coin that rarely comes up yes is rarely tried, so there's almost nothing to learn from that says it should come up more often, and it slides further. The same thing happened to unloading in another brain that night, and its colonies starved. The coins need a floor, a smallest chance of yes, before they're trained any further.

</details>

## How the 0.3 brain was picked

Nine brains trained side by side overnight on rented GPUs, each with its own settings, the weaker ones copying the best every so often and nudging a few settings to try something new. This time each was scored on how many Terrikins were alive at the end, so a colony only scored by growing and keeping its members. 245 rounds of training, 1.95 billion samples, US$5.82 (A$8.29) of rented GPUs.

The population drifted down for hours, as it did once before: members copy each other's latest brain on one noisy score, and a line's best brain isn't kept. Then, near morning, one line climbed. Its best, a brain called y1-28, is the 0.3 brain.

<details>
<summary>The numbers</summary>

64 episodes of six minutes on seeds no training run uses, three colonies of six, room for up to eight each, three nights:

| | Terrikins per colony at the end | seconds alive | colonies lost (of 192) | hatched per colony | died |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the 0.3 brain | 6.40 | 326.3 | 0 | 2.24 | 354 |
| the brain its line started from | 5.95 | 316.3 | 1 | 2.38 | 465 |
| nester (script) | 3.09 | 233.2 | 61 | 0.74 | 701 |

That's 0.45 Terrikins a colony more than the brain it started from, with a quarter fewer deaths, and at their biggest the colonies average 7.33. It's a width-128 brain with 270,349 weights.

## The catch

{{clip colonies-starve}}

Some still starve. Of the 354 Terrikins that died in those tests, 289 died with jelly in their own colony's store. Nearly half died away from home by day: they head out with too little energy, go further than they can get back from, and starve before they turn for home. Most of the rest died in the dark.

Part of that is a rule. A Terrikin can only eat from the store when it's hungry enough, and with the line set low, every trip out was a gamble. I raised it, and fewer die. It's still a rule, though, and it should go once they can learn to trade eating against hatching for themselves.

The other part is memory. Every brain so far decides each moment from only what it senses right then. It can't know where it last saw food, how long it's been out, or that a flower was just over there before it turned round. A lot of what I asked of them this stage needed that, and I tried too much without it.

The whole stage, every experiment and dead end included: 1,115 training runs and about 171 hours of training on my Mac and rented GPUs, for US$20.57 (A$29.29) in rent. Rates from the ECB on 23 October, 1.424.

## Next

Memory, on its own first. I'm adding a small part to the brain that carries something forward from one moment to the next (a GRU), set up so the 0.3 brain acts exactly the same with it to start with, then testing whether it learns to use it in mazes, where you can't get far without remembering where you've been. After that, back to growing: Terrikins that hatch small and grow up on what's brought home.
